Groove Grabber - what each kind of effect is and where it goes. One "@@ type" section per FX type (the ids in
Source/core/Fx.cpp). Plain ASCII, clean words. The FX tab shows the section for the effect you pick.

@@ riser
A sound that climbs in pitch, brightness and volume to build tension. Start it so it ends right on the downbeat of the drop, chorus or hook (a 2 or 4 bar riser before the change is the classic move), then let the drums or an impact take over. Cut it a hair early, or let the beat drop out for the last beat, so the downbeat hits harder.

@@ reverseCymbal
A crash played backwards: it swells up and stops dead. Line its end up exactly with the downbeat of the new section, so the swell sucks you into the chorus. One to two beats long is the usual size. In rock, pop, country and worship it is the most natural transition there is.

@@ swell
A reversed snare, clap or hit that swells into the beat. Shorter than a reverse cymbal: half a beat to a beat. Put its end right on the snare of the next bar, or on the first downbeat of a section, to pull the groove forward.

@@ impact
A big hit with a long tail, the exclamation point of a transition. Put it on the downbeat where the drop, chorus or hook lands, often right after a riser ends. Lay it under the kick and crash rather than in place of them, and leave it room to ring out.

@@ subDrop
A deep boom that falls in pitch, felt more than heard. Hit it on the downbeat of the drop or the hook, usually with the beat stripped back for a beat before. Keep it in tune with the song (or with the 808) so it doesn't fight the low end.

@@ downlifter
A sound that falls in pitch and fades: the opposite of a riser. Start it on the downbeat as a section ends (into a breakdown, a quieter verse or an outro) so the energy drains away over one to four bars.

@@ sweep
Filtered noise or a synth that sweeps up or down. An upward sweep builds into a change like a short riser; a downward sweep winds energy down after a drop. Two to four bars around a section change is typical.

@@ whoosh
A quick rush of air that swells and passes. Let its loudest point land on the downbeat of the change, so it reads as the moment one section swoops into the next. Good for intros, drops and scene changes.

@@ tapeStop
The sound of a tape or turntable slowing to a stop, pitch falling to nothing. Put it on the last beat or two before a drop or a big hook, so the whole track seems to grind to a halt, then hit the drop clean. Use it once or twice a song at most.

@@ rewind
A DJ spin-back or rewind: the record pulled back fast. Classic before the beat restarts, after the intro or before the last hook. Let it end right where the beat comes back in on the one.

@@ crackle
Vinyl crackle and dust. Run it quietly under the whole loop or the intro for a sampled, old-record feel, the signature of boom bap and lo-fi. Keep it low: it should be felt more than noticed, and roll off some low end so it doesn't muddy the kick.

@@ noise
A bed of noise, hiss or room tone. Under the beat it glues things together and adds air; on its own it sets a mood in an intro or a breakdown. Turn it down until you only miss it when it's gone.

@@ scratch
A turntable scratch: the hip-hop accent. Drop one on beat 4 before a turnaround, between vocal lines, or as an answer to the snare. In late 90s rap-rock a scratch on the 4 is a calling card too.

@@ zap
A short electric zap or upward chirp. Use it as an accent between vocal phrases or on an off-beat in the last bar before a change. Electro and trap use them as ear candy; one or two go a long way.

@@ laser
A quick falling laser tone. A retro, playful accent from 80s electro and synth-pop, still used as ear candy in EDM and trap. Put it on the "and" of 4 or as an answer to a vocal ad-lib.

@@ blip
A tiny tonal blip or beep. Sprinkle it on off-beats as ear candy, as a call to a melody, or in a lo-fi intro. Keep it quiet and in key.

@@ glitch
Stutters, chopped and digital artifacts. Use a glitch as a fill in the last half bar before a change, or to chop up a vocal ad-lib. Great in EDM and modern trap; keep them short and rhythmic so they lock to the grid.

@@ horn
An air horn or siren. Big, loud and festive: hit it on the downbeat of the drop or the hook, or at the top of a verse. A trap and dance staple; use it sparingly so it stays an event.

@@ other
An effect that doesn't fall into the usual kinds. Listen to its shape: if it builds, end it on a downbeat; if it hits, start it on one. Right-click it to tell Groove Grabber what it really is.
