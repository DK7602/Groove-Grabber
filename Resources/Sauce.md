@@ hiphop
**The pocket.** Hip-hop drums rest on a heavy kick and a hard backbeat: snare or clap on beats 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), with the kick moving around it. The feel comes from swing and sound, not busy patterns. Some styles are built on sampled breaks, others on drum machines and 808s.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 70 to 110 BPM | 50 to 62% | Backbeat, 8ths or 16ths | Kick, often an 808 under it |

### Kick
- Beat 1 almost always, then hits around beat 3 (steps 9 and 11) and pickups like the & of 4 (step 15).
- Two to four kicks per bar leave room for the bass and the vocal.

### Snare and clap
- Snare on 2 and 4, hit hard and the same every time. It's the anchor.
- Ghost notes (very quiet hits) add bounce. The Dusty Swing groove has one on the last step of bar 2; try others just before a backbeat (step 4 or 12).
- Southern styles stack a clap on the snare. Memphis runs faster (around 136 BPM) in half-time: snare on beat 3 (step 9) only, so the beat feels half as fast.

### Hats and cymbals
- Steady 8th-note hats (every other step). The Dusty Swing groove adds soft 16ths between them, and the swing pushes those late.
- An open hat on the & of 4 (step 15), in bar 2 of the Boom Bap groove, lifts the end of a phrase.

### Percussion and toms
- Tambourine or shaker on the &s (steps 3, 7, 11 and 15) or in 16ths. Toms are mostly for fills.

### 808 and bass
- Boom bap usually has a sampled or played bass line, so keep the kick out of its way.
- With an 808 (the Modern Hip-Hop Knock direction), tune the kick to it and keep the kick short.

### Fills and changes
- Hooks add a layer: a clap, a tambourine, one more kick.
- Drop the kick for a bar before a hook, then come back on beat 1 with a crash.

### Sauce tips
- Swing the 16ths (about 54 to 62%; the grooves here play at 60 and 62%) instead of moving notes by hand.
- Nudge the snare a few milliseconds late for a lazy feel.
- Pick a style above for boom bap, G-funk, Memphis, crunk or old school electro details.

@@ hiphop.east
**The pocket.** East Coast boom bap is the 90s New York sound: punchy drums chopped from old funk and soul breaks, on a swung 16th grid. The beat sits slightly behind the count. DJ Premier and Pete Rock are classic reference points, and samplers like the E-mu SP-1200 and Akai MPC60 shaped the sound.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 85 to 98 BPM | 54 to 62% | Swung 16ths, laid back | Kick and a sampled bass line |

### Kick
- Beat 1, then beat 3 and its & (steps 9 and 11) in the Boom Bap groove.
- Bar 2 shifts it: the a of 2 (step 8), the & of 3 (step 11), and a pickup on the & of 4 (step 15).
- The Dusty Swing groove puts a kick on the & of 2 (step 7) for the classic head-nod stumble.

### Snare and clap
- A fat, cracking snare on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13). Producers often layer two snare samples into one.
- In the Dusty Swing groove, a ghost note (a very quiet hit) on the last step of bar 2 pulls the ear into the next bar.

### Hats and cymbals
- 8th-note hats in the Boom Bap groove. 16th swing leaves them in place and moves only the steps between.
- The Dusty Swing groove adds soft 16ths between the 8ths, and the swing pushes those late: that's where it really shows.
- Louder on the beats, softer between. A flat, robotic hat makes the whole groove stiff.

### Percussion and toms
- Tambourine or shaker on the &s (steps 3, 7, 11 and 15) in the Boom Bap groove, quieter than the hats. Dusty Swing keeps one hit on the & of 3 (step 11) and two soft rims.
- Many beats loop a whole sampled break, like "Funky Drummer" or "Impeach the President", under the hits.

### Fills and changes
- Hooks often add the full break, a second snare layer or a tambourine.
- Drop the drums for a bar before the hook, then slam back on beat 1.

### Sauce tips
- The grooves here swing at 60% (Boom Bap) and 62% (Dusty Swing). Start there and adjust by ear.
- Nudge the snare a few milliseconds late for a lazy pocket.
- Roll off some top end, or bit-crush lightly, for the gritty 12-bit SP-1200 sound.
- Compress the drum bus hard so the room comes up between hits.

@@ hiphop.west
**The pocket.** G-funk is the early 90s West Coast sound, with Dr. Dre, Warren G and DJ Quik as the usual reference points. The drums are clean drum-machine hits, often 808 sounds, with a light swing. The beat sits back and rolls rather than punches, leaving room for synth leads and a funky bass line.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 88 to 100 BPM | 52 to 58% | Laid back, light swing | Kick plus 808 or synth bass |

### Kick
- Beat 1, the & of 2 (step 7) and the & of 3 (step 11): bouncy and syncopated, playing off the bass line.
- Bar 2 moves the last kick to beat 3 (step 9). Keep kicks round, not clicky.
- The G-Funk Machine direction uses the Funk groove (100 BPM, 55% swing): ghost snares on steps 8 and 10, and a kick on the a of 1 (step 4) in bar 2.

### Snare and clap
- Snare and clap together on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13). The clap gives width, the snare gives body.
- A slightly long, roomy clap is part of the sound.

### Hats and cymbals
- 8th-note hats, which the swing leaves in place. Bar 2 adds a medium 16th on the e of 4 (step 14), which it pushes late, and an open hat on the & of 4 (step 15).

### Percussion and toms
- A shaker in 16ths, softer between the 8ths, glues the groove.
- A tambourine on 2 and 4 with the clap is a classic West Coast touch.

### 808 and bass
- The bass is often a synth playing funk lines, with the 808 following the kick under it.
- Tune the kick to the bass's key note.

### Fills and changes
- Verse and hook usually share one loop; the hook adds voices and synths, not drums.
- Use drop-outs: mute the kick for a bar, or leave only hats and clap.

### Sauce tips
- Use a light swing (55 to 57%, as the grooves here play) so the 16th shaker rolls instead of ticking.
- Let the clap be the brightest thing; lower the shaker and hats.
- Nudge the clap a few milliseconds behind the snare.

@@ hiphop.memphis
**The pocket.** 90s Memphis rap was made on drum machines like the Roland TR-808 and passed around on cassette, so the drums sound dark, hissy and a little crushed. The pattern is half-time: snare and clap on beat 3 (step 9) only, so a fast tempo feels slow and heavy. Modern phonk keeps the pattern and adds loud cowbell melodies and distorted 808s.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 125 to 145 BPM | 50% (straight) | Half-time, rolling hats | 808 that follows the kick |

### Kick
- Beat 1, the & of 2 (step 7) and the & of 3 (step 11). Bar 2 adds the a of 1 (step 4).
- Kick and 808 hit together. Use a short, punchy kick on top of a long 808.

### Snare and clap
- Snare and clap together on beat 3 (step 9), once per bar.
- Older tracks used a thin, bright machine snare. Phonk uses a harder, more distorted clap.

### Hats and cymbals
- 8th-note hats with triplet rolls, three quick hits in one step, on the e of 2 (step 6) and the e of 4 (step 14).
- Bar 2 gets busier: triplet bursts on steps 2, 10 and 16, and a quick 32nd double on step 14. Busy hats over a slow backbeat are the signature.

### Percussion and toms
- The cowbell is the star. The grid plays it on steps 1, 4, 7, 9, 12 and 15: a 3-3-2 rhythm twice a bar.
- In phonk the cowbell is often tuned and played as a melody. Toms are rare.

### 808 and bass
- The 808 copies the kick with long notes, tuned to the key. Distort it for phonk.

### Fills and changes
- Drop-outs more than fills: cut the kick and 808 for a bar, leave cowbell and hats, then drop back in.
- Hooks often double the cowbell or add a second cowbell pattern.

### Sauce tips
- Use tape saturation or a lower sample rate for the cassette sound.
- Pitch the snare and cowbell down a few semitones for a darker tone.
- Keep the cowbell velocity even; vary the hats.

@@ hiphop.south
**The pocket.** Southern hip-hop and crunk, at their peak in the 2000s, run on 808-style drum machines: booming kicks, big stacked claps and a slow, heavy tempo. Crunk, with Lil Jon as the best-known producer, is loud, simple and made for crowd chants. Snap music swaps the snare for finger snaps, and Houston's chopped and screwed sound slows whole tracks down.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 70 to 100 BPM | 50 to 54% | Heavy backbeat, 16ths | Booming 808 with the kick |

### Kick
- A skipping kick: beat 1, the a of 1 (step 4), the & of 2 (step 7), beat 3 (step 9) and the a of 3 (step 12).
- Bar 2 swaps the a of 3 for the & of 3 (step 11) and adds beat 4 (step 13), pushing into the snare roll.

### Snare and clap
- Snare and a big, stacked clap on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), often the loudest thing in the mix.
- Bar 2 ends with a snare run: four 16ths on steps 13 to 16.

### Hats and cymbals
- 16th hats, louder on the 8ths, keep a slow tempo moving. Rolls are rarer than in trap.

### Percussion and toms
- A cowbell or perc on the &s (steps 3, 7, 11 and 15).
- New Orleans bounce, a close cousin, is built on the beat from the Showboys' "Drag Rap", sampled over and over.

### 808 and bass
- Long 808 notes on the kick hits, tuned to the key. Let each ring until the next, then cut it.
- For chopped and screwed, slow the whole track down so everything drops in pitch.

### Fills and changes
- Hooks get bigger: more claps, a crash, chants.
- Snare and clap rolls (16ths, then 32nds) build into a hook.

### Sauce tips
- Stack two or three claps with different tones for the big crunk clap.
- Keep the swing low. The weight comes from the sound, not the shuffle.
- Saturate the 808 a little so it shows up on small speakers.
- Make the snare roll get louder toward the downbeat.

@@ hiphop.oldschool
**The pocket.** Old school hip-hop, from the late 70s into the 80s, was built two ways: DJs looping the drum breaks of funk records, and early drum machines like the Roland TR-808 on Afrika Bambaataa's "Planet Rock". Electro took the machine route: straight 16ths, robotic hand claps, booming 808 kicks and cowbell.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 90 to 125 BPM | 50 to 60% | Straight, or swung for breaks | Long 808 kick |

### Kick
- Beat 1 and beat 3 (steps 1 and 9), with an extra kick on the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 1.
- Electro gets more syncopated: try kicks on the a of 2 (step 8) or the & of 4 (step 15).

### Snare and clap
- Snare and clap together on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13). The 808 hand clap is the classic sound.
- Pure electro is machine-tight with no ghost notes. Break-based tracks use a live funk snare.

### Hats and cymbals
- Straight 8th-note hats. Electro often plays accented 16ths.
- A crash on beat 1 marks a new section.

### Percussion and toms
- Cowbell is a must for electro. The grid's perc plays the & of 1 and the & of 3 (steps 3 and 11), plus the & of 4 (step 15) in bar 2.
- 808 toms (low, mid, high) make quick fills and melodic runs.

### 808 and bass
- The 808 copies the kick: one long boom, tuned to the root note.
- With a played bass line, use a shorter kick and leave the 808 out.

### Fills and changes
- DJs looped the break of records like "Apache" for the dancers. The Old School Scratch direction uses the swung Boom Bap groove (90 BPM, 60% swing) for that feel.
- Fills are short: a snare roll, a tom run or a clap burst on beat 4.

### Sauce tips
- Keep electro straight or nearly so (the Old School groove plays at 53%). Machine timing is the point.
- Use real 808 sounds or close copies: long kick, snappy clap, cowbell.
- For break-based tracks, chop a break and play it back at the song's tempo.

@@ trap
**The pocket.** Trap drums run at a fast tempo but feel slow, because the snare or clap only hits on beat 3 (step 9). That's half-time: the groove moves at half the speed of the count. Over it the hats run fast and break into rolls, and a long, tuned 808 carries the bass.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 130 to 160 BPM | 50% (straight) | Half-time, hat rolls | 808 with the kick |

### Kick
- Beat 1, then syncopated hits around beat 3, like the a of 2 (step 8) and the & of 3 (step 11).
- Drill is sparser: the Drill groove kicks on beat 1 and the e of 3 (step 10), then beat 1 and the & of 2 (step 7).

### Snare and clap
- Snare and clap together on beat 3 (step 9), once per bar. Bounce uses the clap alone; Drill adds the & of 4 (step 15) in bar 1.
- Snaps can replace the clap in softer trap.

### Hats and cymbals
- 8th notes (every other step) as the base. Rolls are the signature: two quick 32nds in one step, or a triplet burst of three.
- The Trap groove rolls on steps 6 and 14, then 10 and 12. Drill's hats skip instead (bar 1: steps 1, 4, 7, 9, 12, 15). Switching between 16ths, 32nds and triplets makes the hats talk.

### Percussion and toms
- Sparse perc in the gaps (the Trap groove: steps 4 and 12, then 4 and 13): rims, snaps, FX hits. Toms are rare.

### 808 and bass
- The 808 plays the kick's rhythm with long notes; in Drill it adds its own notes (steps 14 and 13). Tune it to the key, and the kick to the 808.
- To stop clashes, keep the kick short, just the punch on top, and set the 808 to one voice so each note cuts the last.
- Glides: overlap two 808 notes with glide (portamento) on, so the pitch slides into the next.

### Fills and changes
- Intros often run with just hats; the drop brings everything in on beat 1 with a crash.
- Before a drop, mute all but the hats for a bar.

### Sauce tips
- Vary hat velocity (loud, soft, medium) so they don't sound flat.
- Pitch a hat roll up or down for a quick riser.
- Pick a style above for Atlanta, UK drill, NY drill, rage or plugg details.

@@ trap.atlanta
**The pocket.** Atlanta trap is the sound most people mean by "trap": a half-time clap on beat 3, rolling hats, and huge 808s that play the bass line. Zaytoven, Metro Boomin and Southside are good reference points. The drums are hard and bright but leave room for the vocal.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 130 to 150 BPM | 50% (straight) | Half-time, rolling hats | Big 808 with the kick |

### Kick
- Beat 1, then the a of 2 (step 8) and the & of 3 (step 11). Bar 2 uses beat 1, the & of 2 (step 7), the & of 3 and beat 4 (step 13).
- Kicks land near the clap, not on it, so it stays clear.

### Snare and clap
- Clap and snare layered on beat 3 (step 9), once per bar, so the beat feels half as fast as the tempo.
- Short, loud and bright. Some beats add a pickup clap on the a of 4 (step 16).

### Hats and cymbals
- 8th-note hats with rolls: a quick 32nd double on step 6 and a triplet burst on step 14 in bar 1, then doubles on steps 10 and 12 in bar 2. An open hat on the & of 2 (step 7) in bar 2 adds a lift.
- Rolls switch between 16ths, 32nds and triplets. Pitch some roll hits up or down for a "talking" hat.

### Percussion and toms
- Perc in the gaps (steps 4 and 12, then 4 and 13 in bar 2): a rim, snap or vocal chop. Toms are rare.
- The grid's two FX hits mark beat 1 of bar 1 and beat 4 (step 13) of bar 2.

### 808 and bass
- The 808 copies the kick's rhythm with long, tuned notes.
- Keep the kick short and tuned to the 808 so they hit as one. Set the 808 to one voice so each note cuts the last.
- Glide into the next note at phrase ends by overlapping the notes with glide on.

### Fills and changes
- Verses thin out; hooks add rolls, open hats and a crash on beat 1.
- Mute the kick and 808 for a beat before a hook. The silence makes the drop hit.

### Sauce tips
- Vary hat velocity (loud, soft, medium) so the 8ths don't sound flat.
- Saturate the 808 a little so it's heard on phones.
- Keep the clap dry and upfront.

@@ trap.ukdrill
**The pocket.** UK drill grew out of Chicago drill in south London in the early to mid-2010s. It's half-time like trap, but nothing sits square: snares skip off the beat, hats bounce in a triplet-like pattern, and the 808 slides from note to note.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 138 to 145 BPM | 50% (straight) | Half-time, skippy | Sliding 808, sparse kick |

### Kick
- Very sparse: beat 1 in bar 1, then beat 1 and the & of 2 (step 7) in bar 2.
- Some beats let the 808 play alone.

### Snare and clap
- The clap holds beat 3 (step 9). The snare skips around it.
- Bar 1: beat 3 plus a late extra on the & of 4 (step 15). Bar 2: an early hit on the e of 2 (step 6), then beat 3.
- A common move: keep step 9 in one bar and land the snare later in the next (step 10, 11 or 12), so it stumbles.

### Hats and cymbals
- Skippy hats: steps 1, 4, 7, 9, 12 and 15 in bar 1, a 3-3-2 shape played twice.
- Bar 2 adds steps 5 and 14, so it never quite repeats.

### Percussion and toms
- A rim or click on the & of 1 and the & of 3 (steps 3 and 11). Toms are rare.

### 808 and bass
- The 808 has its own rhythm: steps 1, 4 and 11, then 1, 8, 11 and 14.
- Slides are the sound: overlap two notes with glide (portamento) on, and the pitch bends into the next.
- Keep the 808 to one voice. Where kick and 808 meet (beat 1), tune the kick to the 808.

### Fills and changes
- Change the snare skips and 808 slides between sections.
- Drop the drums for a bar and let one 808 slide into the next section.

### Sauce tips
- Leave gaps. The bounce comes from the empty steps as much as the hits.
- Glide time matters: short for quick slides, longer for big swoops.
- Keep the snare short and dry so its placement is clear.

@@ trap.nydrill
**The pocket.** NY drill brought the UK drill pattern to Brooklyn in the late 2010s, with UK producers such as 808Melo making many of the early beats. The rhythm stays close: half-time, skippy hats, sliding 808s. The differences: harder kicks that hit with the 808, and a sample-based tone, with chopped vocal or R&B samples on top.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 138 to 145 BPM | 50% (straight) | Half-time, skippy | Hard kick with sliding 808 |

### Kick
- More kicks than UK drill: beat 1, the & of 2 (step 7) and the & of 3 (step 11), then beat 1, the a of 1 (step 4) and the & of 3.
- Pick a hard, clicky kick that punches through the 808.

### Snare and clap
- Clap on beat 3 (step 9), the half-time backbeat. The snare joins it, plus a late extra on the & of 4 (step 15) in bar 2.
- A common move, as in UK drill: shift the second bar's snare later than step 9.

### Hats and cymbals
- Busy, skippy hats: bar 1 hits steps 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 11, 12, 14 and 16, pairs of 16ths with gaps.
- Bar 2 adds a triplet roll on the e of 3 (step 10) and ends differently, on steps 13, 15 and 16. Keep hats tight and short.

### Percussion and toms
- A perc hit on the e of 2 (step 6) and the & of 4 (step 15). Toms are rare.

### 808 and bass
- The 808 follows the kick with long notes and slides (overlap notes with glide on, one voice).
- Tune the kick to the 808's note so the layer sounds like one hit.
- If the sample has its own bass, filter out its low end.

### Fills and changes
- Let the chopped sample play alone, then drop the full beat on beat 1.
- Drop the kick and 808 for a beat before the hook.

### Sauce tips
- Chop the sample on the grid so its accents line up with the kicks.
- Layer the clap with a sharp snare for more snap.
- Saturate the kick and 808 together so they glue.

@@ trap.rage
**The pocket.** Rage is a 2020s branch of trap built on huge, buzzing synth leads and blown-out 808s. Playboi Carti, Trippie Redd and Ken Carson are common reference points. The drums are fast and driving, with a kick on every beat under a half-time clap on beat 3, all pushed loud and distorted.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 150 to 165 BPM | 50% (straight) | Half-time clap, driving kick | Distorted 808 with the kick |

### Kick
- A kick on every beat (steps 1, 5, 9 and 13), often called four on the floor. Bar 2 adds the & of 3 (step 11).
- Kick and 808 hit together. Distort them as one so the low end is a wall.
- For contrast, drop the kick on beat 2 in a verse.

### Snare and clap
- Snare and clap on beat 3 (step 9) only, the half-time backbeat, so the beat feels half as fast as the tempo.
- Loud, short and distorted, to sit with the synths.

### Hats and cymbals
- Straight 8th-note hats, with quick 32nd doubles in bar 2 (steps 8, 14 and 16).
- Keep rolls short and fast. A big crash marks beat 1 of each section.

### Percussion and toms
- Very little, and none in the grid: one FX hit in a gap is enough. Toms are rare.

### 808 and bass
- The 808 copies the kick, so the bass hits every beat. Tune it to the key and distort it hard.
- With this much distortion, kick and 808 can blur. Keep the kick very short and let the 808 own the lowest notes.

### Fills and changes
- Intros: the synth lead alone, then the full beat on beat 1.
- Verses can drop the steady kick and keep only the 808. Mute everything for a beat right before a drop.

### Sauce tips
- Clip or saturate the drum bus. Rage is meant to sound overdriven.
- Keep the synth lead out of the 808's range.
- Check at low volume: if the kick disappears, shorten the 808 or boost the kick's click.

@@ trap.plugg
**The pocket.** Plugg is a soft, melodic branch of trap from the 2010s, with bouncy 808s, airy synths and simple drums. Pluggnb mixes in R&B chords. The drums use the half-time trap layout but stay gentle: a light clap on beat 3, plain hats and lots of space.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 140 to 160 BPM | 50% (straight) | Half-time, bouncy | Bouncy 808 with the kick |

### Kick
- Few kicks: beat 1 and the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 1; beat 1, the & of 2 (step 7) and the & of 4 (step 15) in bar 2.
- Bar 2 doesn't repeat bar 1. That's the plugg bounce.
- Use a soft, round kick, not a clicky one.

### Snare and clap
- Clap on beat 3 (step 9), once per bar: the half-time backbeat. There's no snare in the grid.
- Soft claps, snaps or a light snare all work.

### Hats and cymbals
- Plain 8th-note hats (every other step). Bar 2 adds a quick 32nd double on step 10 and a triplet burst on the last step.
- An open hat on the & of 2 (step 7) in bar 1 gives a soft lift. Use rolls as accents.

### Percussion and toms
- Two light perc parts: one on beat 2 (step 5), another on the a of 2 and the a of 4 (steps 8 and 16).
- Shakers, snaps and bell-like sounds fit. Toms are rare.

### 808 and bass
- The 808 follows the kick, playing melodic notes that bounce with the synths.
- Glide between notes for a smooth, singing bass: overlap the notes with glide on.
- Keep the kick short and tuned to the 808. Plugg 808s are usually clean, not distorted.

### Fills and changes
- Intros are often synths alone. The drums come in on beat 1 of the hook.
- Verses can drop the hats or 808 for two bars.

### Sauce tips
- Lower the hat velocity and roll off their harsh top end.
- Put a little reverb on the clap for an airy space.
- Leave space for the melody.

@@ lofi
**The pocket.** Lo-fi hip-hop drums are soft, dusty and swung, sitting under warm chords at a relaxed tempo. They borrow boom bap's kick and snare pattern but play it gently, with heavy swing and a loose, slightly late feel. J Dilla's off-grid drums and Nujabes' jazzy beats are the classic reference points.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 70 to 90 BPM | 56 to 64% | Laid back, heavy swing | Soft kick, warm bass |

### Kick
- Beat 1, the a of 2 (step 8) and the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 1; beat 1, beat 3 (step 9) and the & of 3 in bar 2.
- The Jazz Hop direction plays the Dusty Swing groove instead, with a kick on the & of 2 (step 7) in both bars.
- Keep it soft and round. It should thump, not click.

### Snare and clap
- Snare on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), with a ghost note (a very soft hit) on the last step of bar 2.
- Rim clicks, brushed snares and snaps fit too.

### Hats and cymbals
- 16th hats, louder on the 8ths and very soft between. With heavy swing, the soft ones land late and make the lazy shuffle.
- Keep hats dark and quiet.

### Percussion and toms
- A shaker in 16ths, medium and soft in turn, under the hats.
- Ghost rim clicks (beat 1 of bar 1, beat 3 of bar 2) add texture. Toms are rare.

### 808 and bass
- Most lo-fi uses a soft electric or upright bass instead of an 808. The Bedroom Lo-Fi direction adds a gentle 808.
- Don't stack kick and bass on one low note: keep one short.

### Fills and changes
- Lo-fi loops with little change. Drop or filter the drums for a bar to mark a section.
- Fills, if any, are a soft snare pickup or a tape-stop.

### Sauce tips
- Swing hard (both grooves here play at 62%), or play the drums in by hand and don't fully quantize.
- Nudge the snare a little late and keep it softer than the kick.
- Low-pass the drums and add vinyl crackle or tape hiss.
- Pick a style above for jazz hop or lo-fi trap details.

@@ lofi.jazzhop
**The pocket.** Jazz hop and chillhop mix boom bap drums with jazz chords, upright bass and sometimes live-played kits. The drums swing like a jazz drummer, with brushes, rim clicks and ghost notes. Nujabes is the classic reference point.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 80 to 95 BPM | 55 to 62% | Swung, live feel | Soft kick, upright bass |

### Kick
- In the Dusty Swing groove: beat 1, the & of 2 (step 7) and the & of 3 (step 11). Bar 2 trades the & of 3 for beat 3 (step 9) and adds the & of 4 (step 15).
- The Lo-Fi groove, used by two directions, moves bar 1's second kick to the a of 2 (step 8).
- Keep it soft. A jazz kick is felt more than heard.

### Snare and clap
- Snare or brushed snare on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), soft and warm, not cracking.
- Ghost notes (very quiet hits) between backbeats, like the last step of bar 2.
- A rim click (cross-stick) can replace the snare in quiet sections.

### Hats and cymbals
- Swung 16ths: louder 8ths with very soft hits between.
- A ride can replace the hats: every beat, plus the & of 2 and the & of 4 (steps 7 and 15), swung: the classic jazz ride pattern. Swing 8ths for it, since 16th swing leaves steps 7 and 15 straight.

### Percussion and toms
- The Dusty Swing groove adds a perc hit on the & of 3 (step 11) and soft rims on step 15 (bar 1) and step 7 (bar 2).
- Congas and snaps fit. Toms are for gentle fills.

### Fills and changes
- Follow the chords: a soft snare pickup or a cymbal swell into a new section.
- Drop the drums for a bar, leaving keys and bass.
- Switch from hats to ride for a solo or a bridge.

### Sauce tips
- Use brushed-snare samples for an acoustic feel.
- Play the hats in by hand, with slight timing drift.
- Swing around 58 to 62% (both grooves here play at 62%) and nudge the snare a little late.
- Add a little room reverb to the whole kit so it sounds like one drummer.

@@ lofi.trap
**The pocket.** Lo-fi trap, also called sad trap, puts trap drums under soft, hazy chords: a clap on beat 3 (step 9) over an 808, but gentle, filtered and slow. The Bounce groove above has its own tempo of 150 BPM, where that once-a-bar clap feels like 75. The style starts at 75 BPM: there the same grid feels twice as slow, very sleepy and spacious.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 70 to 85, or 140 to 170 BPM | 50 to 56% | Slow half-time, spacious | Soft 808 with the kick |

### Kick
- Few kicks: beat 1 and the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 1; beat 1, the & of 2 (step 7) and the & of 4 (step 15) in bar 2.
- Use a soft, rounded kick, or let the 808 be the kick.

### Snare and clap
- A soft clap on beat 3 (step 9), once per bar. Snaps work well too. A little reverb fills the slow space.
- At 75 BPM, try the clap on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13) and 16th hats: the same feel as the grid at 150.

### Hats and cymbals
- 8th-note hats (every other step). Bar 2 adds a 32nd double on step 10 and a triplet burst on the last step.
- An open hat on the & of 2 (step 7) in bar 1 gives a soft lift. Keep hats dark and low.

### Percussion and toms
- A light perc on beat 2 (step 5), like a snap or shaker. The grid's second perc, on the a of 2 and the a of 4 (steps 8 and 16), can stay quiet or go. Toms are rare.

### 808 and bass
- The 808 follows the kick with long, soft notes. Tune it to the key, and the kick to the 808.
- Use a clean, sine-like 808, or roll off its upper harmonics, for a softer tone. Slow glides suit the mood.

### Fills and changes
- Drop or filter the drums for a bar to mark a new section.
- A tape-stop or a reversed cymbal makes a soft transition.

### Sauce tips
- Low-pass the drum bus a little for the hazy sound.
- Add light tape wobble or vinyl noise.
- Keep hats and claps quieter than in regular trap. The chords lead.

@@ rnb
**The pocket.** R&B drums sit under the voice: a smooth, steady groove, a soft but clear snare on 2 and 4, and gentle 16th hats. 90s R&B swings like hip-hop, while modern R&B borrows the half-time bounce and long 808s of trap.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 65 to 100 BPM | 50 to 62% | Laid back, 16ths | Kick with an 808 or bass under it |

### Kick
- The R&B grid plays beat 1, the a of 1 (step 4) and the & of 3 (step 11), adding beat 3 (step 9) in bar 2: light and bouncy, never crowding the vocal.
- The Boom Bap grid (90s R&B) opens with a kick on beat 1, beat 3 and the & of 3 (steps 1, 9, 11).
- In the Bounce grid (Modern R&B) the kick and 808 hit together and leave big gaps.

### Snare and clap
- Backbeat on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), often a snare with a finger snap on top.
- Modern R&B goes half-time: one clap on beat 3 (step 9) only, so the song feels half as fast. The Bounce grid plays at 150 BPM, which feels like 75.
- Soft rim clicks on the & of 2 and the & of 4 (steps 7 and 15) are ghost notes: hits so quiet you feel them more than hear them.

### Hats and cymbals
- 16th hats, 8ths louder and the in-between notes soft.
- Modern R&B adds quick hat rolls and the odd open hat on the & of 2 (step 7).

### Percussion and toms
- A 16th shaker, snaps on 2 and 4, and now and then a conga or triangle. Toms are rare.

### 808 and bass
- In the R&B grid the 808 follows the main kicks (steps 1 and 11, then 1 and 9 in bar 2) and skips the pickup on step 4. Let it ring and tune it to the key.

### Fills and changes
- Fills are small: a snare pickup, a hat roll, a reverse cymbal into the hook.
- Drop the kick for the last bar before the hook, then bring everything back on beat 1.

### Sauce tips
- Keep velocities gentle. The drums should never shout over the singer.
- Nudge snares and snaps a few milliseconds late for a relaxed feel.
- Keep the kick dry and give the snare a short reverb.
- Pick a style above for new jack swing, neo soul, alt R&B or funk and Motown.

@@ rnb.newjack
**The pocket.** New jack swing, from the late 80s and early 90s, put hard drum machine beats under R&B singing. The 16ths swing heavily, the snare snaps hard on 2 and 4, and the kick is busy and bouncy. Teddy Riley is the producer most tied to the sound. Slow jams and quiet storm are the slower, softer ballad side.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 98 to 115 BPM (slow jams 60 to 80) | 58 to 66% | Swung 16ths | Punchy machine kick, synth bass |

### Kick
- The New Jack Swing grid plays beat 1, beat 2, the & of 2 and the & of 3 (steps 1, 5, 7, 11). The kick under the snare on beat 2 is part of the bounce.
- Bar 2 adds a kick on the e of 4 (step 14) to push into the next bar.

### Snare and clap
- A hard, bright snare layered with a clap on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13).
- Ghost snares (very soft hits) on the a of 2 (step 8), and on steps 15 and 16 at the end of bar 2, lead back to the top.

### Hats and cymbals
- Swung 16th hats with the 8ths louder. The swing is what makes it new jack: the grid plays at 65%, so the in-between 16ths land late, almost on a triplet.
- Open hats are rare. A crash on beat 1 marks a new section.

### Percussion and toms
- A snap, shaker or tambourine on the & of each beat (steps 3, 7, 11, 15) bounces against the snare.
- Slow jams like a soft shaker, finger snaps and a gentle ride.

### Fills and changes
- Fills are quick swung snare rolls, or a stop: everything cuts out for a beat while the singer carries it.
- Strip the verse back, then add the clap layer and an extra kick for the hook.

### Sauce tips
- Use heavy swing, 60 to 66% (the grid's 65% is a good start), on every lane, not just the hats.
- Keep the snare loud and the ghosts very quiet, so the backbeat stands out.
- Pick crisp, slightly gritty drum machine sounds, like an old 12-bit sampler.
- For a slow jam, use the R&B grid (76 BPM, 58% swing), slow it to 65 or so if you like, and soften every hit.

@@ rnb.neosoul
**The pocket.** Neo soul drums feel like a live drummer leaning back in the chair. The hits land a little behind the beat, the hats swing loosely, and quiet ghost notes fill the gaps. D'Angelo's records with Questlove on drums and J Dilla's off-grid programming are the classic reference points.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 70 to 95 BPM | 58 to 66%, or loose | Behind the beat | Soft round kick, live bass |

### Kick
- The R&B grid (Neo Soul Pocket) plays beat 1, the a of 1 (step 4) and the & of 3 (step 11). The Dusty Swing grid plays beat 1, the & of 2 and the & of 3 (steps 1, 7, 11).
- Keep it soft and round. It talks with the bass player rather than locking to a grid.
- Change the second bar a little. Real drummers rarely play two bars the same.

### Snare and clap
- Backbeat on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), relaxed and fat. A cross-stick (rim click) suits quieter songs.
- Ghost notes are key: soft rim taps on the & of 2 or the & of 4 (steps 7 and 15), and in the Dusty Swing grid a soft snare on the last 16th of bar 2 (step 16) leading back to the top.

### Hats and cymbals
- 16th hats, 8ths louder and the in-between notes barely touched.
- Let the hats drift a little. A dark, dry ride can take over in the chorus.

### Percussion and toms
- Congas, a shaker and a triangle add a warm, live layer. Keep them low in the mix.
- An occasional small tom fill sounds natural. Avoid big rock fills.

### Fills and changes
- Changes are subtle: open the hats a little, add a ride, or let the kick double up in the chorus.
- Fills are short snare-and-ghost runs or a single tom hit.

### Sauce tips
- Nudge the snare 10 to 25 milliseconds late against the kick and hats.
- Quantize lightly, or play the parts in by hand and keep the mistakes that feel good.
- Use three velocity levels: accent, normal and ghost.
- Pick dusty, warm samples and roll off a little top end.

@@ rnb.alt
**The pocket.** Alt R&B, with offshoots like trap soul and dark R&B, takes the 808s and hat rolls of trap and slows everything down. The drums are sparse, with lots of empty space for reverb, pads and vocals. The Weeknd, Frank Ocean and Bryson Tiller are good reference points.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 60 to 80 BPM | 50 to 58% | Half-time, sparse | Long 808 doing the bass |

### Kick
- The Bounce grid (Trap Soul) plays beat 1 and the & of 3 (step 11), then beat 1, the & of 2 and the & of 4 (steps 1, 7, 15) in bar 2.
- Leave beats empty. A missing kick matters as much as a played one.
- A short, clicky kick on top of the 808 helps it come through on small speakers.

### Snare and clap
- Half-time: one clap or snap on beat 3 (step 9) per bar, so the song feels slow and heavy.
- The R&B grid (Dark and Airy) keeps the snare on 2 and 4 at a slow 76 BPM with light swing. The Bounce grid runs at 150: a clap on beat 3 at 150 feels the same as a snare on 2 and 4 at 75.

### Hats and cymbals
- 8th or 16th hats with the odd roll: a 32nd-note double or a triplet roll near the end of a phrase, as in bar 2 of the Bounce grid (steps 10 and 16).
- One open hat on the & of 2 (step 7) is plenty. Keep crashes to beat 1 of a new section, as in the Bounce grid.

### Percussion and toms
- In the Bounce grid, one perc hit on beat 2 (step 5) and others on the a of 2 and the a of 4 (steps 8 and 16) keep the space moving. Toms are rare.

### 808 and bass
- The 808 is the bass line. It hits with the kick, rings long and often slides between notes. Tune it to the key.

### Fills and changes
- Change by taking away: drop the drums in the pre-chorus, then bring everything back for the hook.
- Short hat rolls and a reverse cymbal are the main fills.

### Sauce tips
- Keep the hats quiet and vary their velocity so rolls fade in and out.
- Put a long, dark reverb on the snap or clap, and keep the kick dry.
- Use fewer sounds than you think. Space is the style.

@@ rnb.funk
**The pocket.** Motown's house band, the Funk Brothers, built hits on a steady backbeat with tambourine on top. Funk, as James Brown's bands played it, leans on "the one": the first beat of the bar is the strongest, and the rest is tight, busy 16ths with ghost notes. Clyde Stubblefield's "Funky Drummer" break is the textbook example.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 95 to 125 BPM | 50 to 56% | Tight 16ths, on the one | Live kick locked to the bass |

### Kick
- The Motown grid keeps it simple: beats 1 and 3 (steps 1 and 9).
- The Funk grid syncopates: steps 1, 7, 11 and 13 (beat 1, the & of 2, the & of 3, beat 4), then steps 1, 4, 7 and 11 in bar 2.
- Always hit beat 1 firmly. That is the one.
- The Disco grid (Disco Funk) goes four on the floor: a kick on every beat.

### Snare and clap
- Backbeat on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), crisp and dry. A common Motown move: the snare on all four beats, doubled by the tambourine.
- Funk adds ghost notes (soft taps between the loud hits) on the a of 2 and the e of 3 (steps 8 and 10), plus the & of 4 (step 15) in bar 2.

### Hats and cymbals
- Motown: steady 8th hats. Funk: 16th hats with the 8ths accented, and an open hat on the & of 4 (step 15) that closes on the next beat. Disco: an open hat on every & (steps 3, 7, 11, 15).

### Percussion and toms
- Tambourine is the Motown sound. Play it on every beat, like the Motown grid's perc lane, or only on 2 and 4 with the snare.
- Congas and cowbell suit funk. The Funk grid's perc plays a syncopated part on steps 3, 6, 11 and 14.

### 808 and bass
- No 808. The kick and bass guitar lock together, often on the same syncopated 16ths.

### Fills and changes
- Funk often drops to just drums for a break, then the band comes back on the one.
- Motown fills are short snare pickups into the chorus, with a crash on beat 1.

### Sauce tips
- Use a wide velocity range: loud backbeats, very quiet ghosts.
- Keep timing tight but human, with only a few milliseconds of drift.
- Warm, roomy sounds with tape-style saturation give 60s and 70s color.

@@ pop
**The pocket.** Pop drums serve the hook. They are steady and clean, with a backbeat on 2 and 4 and a clear lift from verse to chorus. The sounds change with each era, but the job stays the same: keep people moving and leave the vocal on top.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 95 to 125 BPM | 50 to 54% | Straight 8ths and 16ths | Tight kick, synth or bass guitar |

### Kick
- The Pop grid plays beat 1, the & of 2 and beat 3 (steps 1, 7, 9), adding beat 4 (step 13) in bar 2.
- The Big Room and Synth Pop grids keep it to beats 1 and 3, with an extra kick on the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 2.
- Modern pop often swaps in an 808, or goes four on the floor (a kick on every beat) for dance songs.

### Snare and clap
- Snare and clap together on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), as in the Pop grid. The clap adds width and snap. The Synth Pop grid uses the snare alone.
- Big ballads and 80s pop use one huge snare with a long or gated reverb.

### Hats and cymbals
- 8th hats are the default. The Big Room grid plays quarter-note hats with an open hat on every & (steps 3, 7, 11, 15), stopping after step 7 in bar 2 for the fill.
- A crash on beat 1 marks each new section.

### Percussion and toms
- A shaker in 16ths fills the space in the Pop grid. Tambourine on 2 and 4 lifts a chorus.
- The Big Room and Synth Pop grids end bar 2 with a tom fill down from beat 4: high, mid, then two low hits (steps 13 to 16).

### Fills and changes
- Verse: fewer parts, often just kick, snare and a shaker. Pre-chorus: build with a snare roll or rising hats. Chorus: everything, plus a crash.
- Drop the drums for a bar before the last chorus, then bring them all back on beat 1.

### Sauce tips
- Layer the clap a few milliseconds after the snare for a wider backbeat.
- Keep the kick and snare at full velocity and vary only the hats and shaker.
- Pick a style above for synth pop, dance pop, pop rock or indie pop.

@@ pop.synth
**The pocket.** 80s synth pop drums are machine-made and proud of it. Drum machines like the LinnDrum, the Oberheim DMX and the Roland TR-808 play strict, straight patterns, and the snare is often made huge with gated reverb: a big burst of room sound that cuts off suddenly. Phil Collins made that sound famous on "In the Air Tonight". Synthwave and retrowave bring it back today.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 to 130 BPM | 50% (straight) | Straight 8ths, on the grid | Machine kick, synth bass |

### Kick
- The grid plays beats 1 and 3 (steps 1 and 9), with an extra kick on the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 2.
- New wave and synthwave often go four on the floor (a kick on every beat), or add 8th-note kicks under a pulsing bass.
- Keep the kick short and punchy so the synth bass has room.

### Snare and clap
- A big snare on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13) with gated reverb. Layer a clap for a brighter, wider hit.
- Electronic kits like the Simmons gave the era its hollow, synthetic toms and snares.

### Hats and cymbals
- Straight 8th hats, very even. Bar 2 drops them after the & of 3 (step 11) to make room for the fill.
- A crash with a long reverb starts each section.

### Percussion and toms
- A 16th shaker or tambourine with soft in-between notes gives a sequencer-like motion.
- The grid ends bar 2 with a tom fill: high, mid, then two low hits (steps 13 to 16). Big, gated toms are classic 80s.

### Fills and changes
- Fills are tom runs and snare rolls, often with the same gated reverb.
- Choruses add the clap layer, open hats or a tambourine. Bridges often drop to just kick and synths.

### Sauce tips
- Quantize everything. The machine feel is the point.
- Gate the snare reverb so it stops after about a quarter note.
- Keep velocities even, like an early drum machine.
- Add a little stereo chorus or delay to the hats for shine.

@@ pop.dance
**The pocket.** Dance pop runs on four on the floor: a kick on every beat, so the song never stops pushing. Claps snap on 2 and 4, open hats sit on the off-beats between the kicks, and the bass ducks out of the kick's way. It is club music shaped into a radio song, from 90s Eurodance to the disco-flavored pop of today.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 115 to 130 BPM | 50 to 54% | Straight, four on the floor | Big kick, bass ducking under it |

### Kick
- Every beat (steps 1, 5, 9 and 13), all at the same strong velocity.
- Pick a short, punchy kick with a clear click. A long boom blurs at this tempo.

### Snare and clap
- Clap and snare together on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13). The clap is usually the main voice.
- Snare rolls build tension before a chorus: 8ths, then 16ths, then 32nds.

### Hats and cymbals
- Open hat on the & of every beat (steps 3, 7, 11 and 15). This off-beat hat is the classic dance sound.
- Closed hats in 16ths. In the Dance Pop grid the e and a (the in-between notes) are louder than the 8ths; the Disco grid does the opposite.
- A crash on beat 1 of each new section.

### Percussion and toms
- A 16th shaker with soft in-between notes adds drive.
- The Disco grid (Disco Pop) adds a second perc, such as congas, on the a of 1 and the & of 3 (steps 4 and 11), plus the e of 4 (step 14) in bar 2.
- Toms are rare.

### 808 and bass
- The bass usually plays on the off-beats, or ducks under each kick with sidechain compression. That gives the pumping sound.

### Fills and changes
- Verse: kick and claps, light hats. Chorus: all the hats, shaker and crash.
- Pre-chorus: drop the kick and build with a snare roll and a rising noise sweep.
- Bridge: a breakdown with no kick for 4 or 8 bars.

### Sauce tips
- Keep the kick at full velocity and dry; put the reverb on the claps.
- Layer two claps a few milliseconds apart for a wide, thick hit.
- Tune the kick to the key of the song.

@@ pop.rock
**The pocket.** Pop rock is a live drummer playing a clean, strong rock beat under a big pop hook. The backbeat on 2 and 4 is loud and steady, the kick follows the guitars and bass, and the chorus opens up with crashes or a ride. It ranges from bright power pop to big arena anthems.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 110 to 150 BPM | 50% (straight) | Straight 8ths, driving | Live kick with bass guitar |

### Kick
- The Rock grid plays beat 1, the & of 2 and beat 3 (steps 1, 7, 9), adding the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 2.
- The Arena grid plays beat 1, beat 3 and the & of 3 (steps 1, 9, 11), then just beats 1 and 3 under the fill in bar 2.
- Lock the kick with the bass guitar's main notes. In a driving chorus, play it in 8ths or quarters under the guitars.

### Snare and clap
- A loud, full snare on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13). Play it the same every time; consistency is the sound.
- Claps are rare. Add a little room sound to the snare instead.

### Hats and cymbals
- Verses: 8th closed hats. Choruses: open the hats a little, or move to the ride, as in the Arena grid.
- A crash on beat 1 of each new section, and on big accents.

### Percussion and toms
- Tambourine on 2 and 4, like the Rock grid's perc lane, lifts the chorus.
- The Arena grid ends bar 2 with a 16th-note tom fill from the & of 3 (step 11): two hits each on the high, mid and low toms.

### Fills and changes
- Fills come at the end of 4 or 8 bars, most often in the last beat or two.
- Pre-chorus: build with 8th or quarter-note snares. Bridge: drop to toms, or to half-time (snare on beat 3 only), then crash back in.
- Last chorus: everything, with extra crashes.

### Sauce tips
- Use a live kit or multi-sampled drums with several velocity layers, so repeats don't sound robotic.
- Keep the backbeat loud and the hats a bit softer and varied.
- Add room mics or room reverb for size, and compress it hard.

@@ pop.indie
**The pocket.** Indie and bedroom pop drums are soft, warm and a little imperfect. They often come from a cheap drum machine, a sampled kit or a small live kit in a room, and they sit back in the mix behind dreamy guitars, synths and soft vocals. The groove is simple; the character comes from the sounds.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 80 to 120 BPM | 50 to 56% | Relaxed, straight or light swing | Soft kick, bass guitar or synth |

### Kick
- The grid plays beat 1, the & of 2 and beat 3 (steps 1, 7, 9), with beat 4 (step 13) added in bar 2.
- Use a soft, round kick, not a clicky one. Some songs use just a kick on 1 and 3.

### Snare and clap
- Snare and clap on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13). Try a soft snare, a rim click, a finger snap or a roomy clap.
- Dream pop washes the snare in long reverb.

### Hats and cymbals
- 8th hats, played gently. A tambourine or shaker can replace the hat.
- Crashes are soft and few. A swelling cymbal often works better than a hard crash.

### Percussion and toms
- A 16th shaker with lighter in-between notes, as in the grid, is the heart of a lot of bedroom pop.
- Tambourine on 2 and 4 for a lift. Soft floor toms for a moody verse.

### Fills and changes
- Keep fills simple: a few toms, a snare flam (two hits almost together), or just a cymbal swell.
- Many songs start with no drums or a single drum machine loop, then bring in the full kit for the chorus.
- Try a half-time bridge, with the snare on beat 3 (step 9) only.

### Sauce tips
- Roll off some top end and add tape-style saturation for a cozy, lo-fi tone.
- Nudge some hits a few milliseconds off the grid, or play them in by hand.
- Old drum machine sounds played dead straight suit bedroom pop.
- Sit the drums a little lower in the mix than you would for radio pop.

@@ edm
**The pocket.** EDM drums are built for big speakers and dance floors. Most of it is four on the floor: a kick on every beat, claps on 2 and 4, and open hats on the off-beats. Tracks are shaped around builds and drops: the drums pull back, then the full beat lands. Dubstep and future bass use half-time instead.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 120 to 130 BPM (most styles) | 50 to 56% | Straight, four on the floor | Big kick, bass ducking under it |

### Kick
- The Festival and House grids put the kick on every beat (steps 1, 5, 9, 13).
- The kick is the loudest thing in the track. Pick one with a clear click and a solid low thump.
- The Bounce grid (Future Bass) is half-time at 150 BPM, so it feels like 75. Its kick and 808 play beat 1 and the & of 3 (step 11), then steps 1, 7 and 15 in bar 2.

### Snare and clap
- Claps on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), often layered with a snare.
- Half-time styles move the clap to beat 3 (step 9).

### Hats and cymbals
- In the Festival and House grids, an open hat on the & of each beat (steps 3, 7, 11, 15). Closed hats in 8ths (Festival) or 16ths (House) fill around it.
- Rides add drive in techno and big drops. A crash marks the drop.

### Percussion and toms
- Shakers and percussion fill the e and a of each beat, as in the Festival grid.
- Toms are rare.

### 808 and bass
- The kick and bass must not fight. Sidechain the bass to the kick so it ducks on every hit, or write the bass on the off-beats.

### Fills and changes
- Build: a snare roll that speeds up, a noise riser, no kick. Drop: everything, with a crash on beat 1.
- Breakdowns remove the kick for 8 or 16 bars.
- The Festival grid's FX lanes show this: an impact on beat 1 and a second FX hit on beat 4 of bar 2.

### Sauce tips
- Keep the kick at full velocity and vary the hats.
- Check the low end in mono so the kick and bass stay solid.
- Put a short reverb on the claps and none on the kick.
- Pick a style above for house, techno, drum and bass, dubstep or disco.

@@ edm.house
**The pocket.** House grew out of Chicago clubs in the 80s, built on drum machines like the Roland TR-909 and TR-808. The kick plays every beat, a clap hits 2 and 4, and an open hat on every off-beat gives the classic push. Light swing on the 16ths makes it groove instead of march. Frankie Knuckles is often called the Godfather of House.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 118 to 128 BPM | 52 to 58% | Four on the floor, swung 16ths | Round kick, off-beat bass |

### Kick
- Every beat: steps 1, 5, 9 and 13. Deep house uses a softer, rounder kick; tech house a tighter, punchier one.
- Keep it steady and dry. The hats and percussion do the moving.

### Snare and clap
- Clap on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13). A 909-style clap or a layered handclap is the classic choice.

### Hats and cymbals
- Open hat on the & of every beat (steps 3, 7, 11, 15). This is the main house groove.
- Closed hats in 16ths, 8ths louder and the in-between notes soft, with light swing.
- A ride in 8ths or quarters lifts a peak section.

### Percussion and toms
- The House grid adds perc hits on the a of 1, the & of 3 and the a of 3 (steps 4, 11 and 12). Shakers, congas, bongos and tambourine all fit.
- The Disco House direction uses the Disco grid, with busier 16th percussion.

### 808 and bass
- The bass often plays on the off-beats, between kicks, so the two never clash. Sidechain it to the kick for a gentle pump.

### Fills and changes
- House changes in 8, 16 or 32 bar blocks. Bring parts in and out: hats, then claps, then percussion.
- Breakdowns drop the kick and leave hats and pads. A clap or snare roll builds back to the drop.
- Fills are small: a skipped kick, an extra clap or a crash.

### Sauce tips
- The House grid swings its 16ths lightly, at 53%. Try 54 to 58% on the hats and percussion for a looser, deeper groove.
- For UK garage, raise the tempo to about 130 to 135, skip some kicks for a 2-step feel, and swing harder.
- Keep the kick and bass in mono and spread the hats and percussion wide.

@@ edm.techno
**The pocket.** Techno is hypnotic and machine-driven. A steady kick on every beat, off-beat hats and rides, and small changes over long stretches keep people dancing. It started in Detroit in the 80s with Juan Atkins, Derrick May and Kevin Saunderson, and grew darker and harder in Berlin and across Europe. The Roland TR-909 is its classic drum machine.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 125 to 140 BPM | 50 to 54% | Straight, driving, repetitive | Kick with a low rumble under it |

### Kick
- Every beat (steps 1, 5, 9, 13). The kick is the lead instrument, so take time picking it.
- Minimal techno uses a short, dry kick. Industrial and warehouse techno use a distorted kick with a rumble: a reverb tail, filtered low and ducked by the next kick.

### Snare and clap
- Claps on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), as in the Festival grid, or rim clicks instead. Many techno tracks leave them out for long stretches.

### Hats and cymbals
- Open hat on the & of every beat (steps 3, 7, 11, 15), with closed hats in 8ths or 16ths around it.
- A ride on the off-beats, or in 8ths, is a classic techno layer. Let it ring.

### Percussion and toms
- The grid's perc plays on the e and a of each beat (the even steps). Use metallic clicks, short noise hits or a shaker.
- Toms show up as repeating patterns, often filtered, rather than as fills.

### 808 and bass
- The kick and bass share the low end. Let the kick be the bass, or put a short bass note on each off-beat.

### Fills and changes
- Changes happen slowly: filter the hats open, add a ride, mute the kick for a bar. Fills are rare.
- Breakdowns strip to percussion and atmosphere, then the kick returns on beat 1.

### Sauce tips
- Change one small thing every 8 or 16 bars: velocity, filter, decay or a new perc.
- Saturate the kick and keep it in mono.
- Detroit techno is warmer and more musical; industrial is harsher. Pick sounds to match.

@@ edm.dnb
**The pocket.** Drum and bass is fast, around 170 to 175 BPM, but the bass and chords often move at half that speed, so you can nod along at about 87. The basic beat is the two-step: kick on 1, snare on 2, kick on the & of 3, snare on 4. It grew out of jungle, from the UK in the early 90s. Jungle producers chopped up old funk breaks such as the Amen break, from the Winstons' "Amen, Brother".

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 170 to 175 BPM | 50 to 54% | Fast breakbeat, two-step | Short kick, deep sub bass |

### Kick
- The grid plays beat 1 and the & of 3 (steps 1 and 11), adding the e of 4 (step 14) in bar 2.
- Keep the kick short and punchy so the sub bass can fill the low end.
- Rollers keep one kick pattern; jungle moves it around with the break.

### Snare and clap
- Snare on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13): loud, bright and sharp.
- Bar 2 adds a snare on the a of 2 (step 8), a typical break-style push.
- Try quiet ghost snares between the main hits to make it roll.

### Hats and cymbals
- 8th hats with a shaker on the in-between 16ths (the even steps), as in the grid.
- Rides are common in liquid DnB. Crashes mark the drop.

### Percussion and toms
- Jungle layers a chopped break over, or instead of, the programmed drums. Tambourine and small percussion add speed.

### 808 and bass
- The sub bass holds long notes that fill the gaps between kicks. Neurofunk uses a thick, detuned Reese bass; liquid a smooth, warm one.

### Fills and changes
- Intros often run drums with no bass for 16 or 32 bars, so DJs can mix in.
- The drop brings in the full beat and bass on beat 1. Breakdowns strip to hats or nothing.
- Fills: snare rolls, chopped break edits and quick stutters.

### Sauce tips
- Layer a programmed kick and snare over a break, and set the break quieter.
- Cut a break into single hits and rearrange them on the grid.
- Filter the low end out of the break so it doesn't muddy the kick.

@@ edm.dubstep
**The pocket.** Dubstep runs at about 140 BPM but feels like 70, because it is half-time: the snare hits only on beat 3 (step 9) of each bar. That leaves huge gaps for the bass. It began in South London in the early 2000s and grew into louder brostep and minimal, repetitive riddim. Skrillex brought the heavy style to a wide audience.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| About 140 BPM | 50 to 56% | Half-time | Sub bass and wobble bass |

### Kick
- The grid plays only beat 1, then beat 1 and the & of 4 (step 15) in bar 2. Space is the point.
- For a busier drop, add a kick on step 3 or 12, but keep beat 3 clear for the snare.

### Snare and clap
- One huge snare on beat 3 (step 9). Layer a snare, a clap and some noise for size.
- Riddim keeps it dry and tight. Melodic dubstep adds a long reverb.

### Hats and cymbals
- 8th hats keep time. Early UK dubstep often used shuffled or triplet hats.
- The grid ends bar 2 with 32nd-note doubles on the e and a of 4 (steps 14 and 16).
- A crash on beat 1 of the drop.

### Percussion and toms
- Small percussion: rim clicks, shakers, metallic hits. Keep it sparse.
- Toms show up in fills and builds, not in the groove.

### 808 and bass
- The bass is the star. Let the sub hold long notes, and write the wobble or growl bass in the gaps around the snare.
- Duck the bass under the kick and snare so they come through.

### Fills and changes
- Build: a snare roll that speeds up (8ths, 16ths, 32nds) and a rising noise sweep, then a short silence before the drop.
- The grid's FX lanes show an impact on beat 1 and a second FX hit on beat 4 of bar 2.

### Sauce tips
- Make the snare very loud. It carries the whole half-time feel.
- Cut every drum for a beat before the drop and let only a reverb tail ring.
- Use hat rolls and triplets to add energy without crowding the bass.

@@ edm.disco
**The pocket.** Disco is a live band playing four on the floor. The kick hits every beat, the snare and clap hit 2 and 4, and an open hi-hat on every & gives the famous off-beat lift. Real drummers played it, so it breathes and pushes a little. Earl Young of the Philadelphia soul sound is often credited with the pattern. Italo disco (80s) and nu-disco (today) keep the feel but lean on drum machines and synths.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 110 to 130 BPM | 50 to 55% | Four on the floor, live | Steady kick, bouncing octave bass |

### Kick
- Every beat (steps 1, 5, 9, 13). Steady, round and not too loud.
- Disco drummers rarely change it. The groove comes from everything around the kick.

### Snare and clap
- Snare and clap together on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13). Boogie leans on the clap, 70s disco on a bright snare.
- Add soft ghost snares between the backbeats for a funkier, live feel.

### Hats and cymbals
- Open hat on the & of every beat (steps 3, 7, 11, 15), closing on the next beat.
- Closed hats in 16ths, with the 8ths louder and the in-between notes soft.
- A crash on beat 1 of each section.

### Percussion and toms
- A tambourine or shaker in 16ths runs through the whole grid.
- A second perc, such as congas, plays the a of 1 and the & of 3 (steps 4 and 11), adding the e of 4 (step 14) in bar 2.
- Italo disco often uses pitched electronic toms.

### 808 and bass
- The bass often bounces in octaves on 8ths: low on the beat with the kick, high on the &.

### Fills and changes
- Breakdowns drop to just kick and hats, or just percussion, then the snare and the band come back.
- Fills are short snare rolls or tom runs in the last beat of a phrase.

### Sauce tips
- Push the open hats a few milliseconds early for more drive.
- Keep velocities human: vary the hats and percussion slightly every bar.
- Use warm, roomy samples for 70s disco and cleaner machine sounds for nu-disco and Italo.

@@ rock
**The pocket.** Rock drums are built on the backbeat: a hard snare on 2 and 4 that the band leans on. The kick follows the bass and the riff, and the hats or ride count steady 8ths. Keep the small differences in timing and loudness so it sounds like a real drummer.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 to 140 BPM | 50% (straight) | Straight 8ths, backbeat on 2 and 4 | Kick locked with the bass guitar |

### Kick
- The core is beat 1 (step 1) and beat 3 (step 9). The Rock groove adds a push on the & of 2 (step 7), plus the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 2; the Arena groove pushes the & of 3 in bar 1.
- Lock the kick to the bass guitar, and add 8th-note kicks in the chorus for drive.

### Snare and clap
- Snare on beat 2 (step 5) and beat 4 (step 13), hit hard and in the center.
- Ghost notes (very quiet snare taps between backbeats) add groove to bluesy rock.
- Claps are rare. A tambourine on 2 and 4 (the Rock groove's Perc 1 lane) thickens the backbeat instead.

### Hats and cymbals
- Closed hats on 8ths in the verse, then the ride or a half-open, washy hat in the chorus.
- Crash on the 1 of every new section, with a kick under it.
- Accent the hats on the beat, softer on the &s.

### Percussion and toms
- Tambourine on 2 and 4 or 8ths, shaker for quiet verses, a little cowbell.
- Toms live in fills, though the floor tom can replace the hats for a pounding verse.

### Fills and changes
- The Arena groove's bar 2 is the classic fill: 16ths down the toms from the & of 3 (step 11), high to floor, landing on the crash at the top of the loop.
- Fill across the last bar or two beats before a chorus; one-beat fills for small turnarounds.
- For a breakdown, drop to kick and hats, then return on a crash.

### Sauce tips
- Don't quantize everything to 100%. Try 80 to 90%, with kick and snare tight and hats looser.
- Vary hat velocity so no two hats are exactly the same level.
- Room sound is half of rock: blend in a hard-compressed room mic or reverb.
- Pick a style above for classic, hard rock, metal, alt or punk patterns.

@@ rock.classic
**The pocket.** Classic rock from the 60s and 70s is loose, warm and human. Bands usually played together without a click, so the tempo breathes and the snare often sits a little behind the beat. Think of Ringo Starr, Charlie Watts and John Bonham: parts that serve the song, a lot of feel, a real room.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 95 to 130 BPM | 50 to 54% (blues shuffles up to 66%) | Straight or shuffled 8ths | Kick with the bass, round and open |

### Kick
- Beat 1 and beat 3, with pushes on the & of 2 (step 7) or the & of 3 (step 11) that follow the riff. The Dry and Blues kits use the Rock groove, the Room and Stadium kits the Arena groove.
- Fewer kicks than modern rock, to leave room for the bass.

### Snare and clap
- Backbeat on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13). 60s snares tend to be thinner and brighter; 70s snares are often damped, fat and dry.
- In blues rock, add a soft ghost note just before the backbeat (step 4 or step 12).

### Hats and cymbals
- Hats on 8ths with lots of velocity change, sometimes half open and sloshy. Loud sections ride the crash or the ride.
- For a shuffle, swing the 8ths: move every & late toward a triplet feel.
- Crash on the 1 of each section.

### Percussion and toms
- Tambourine on 2 and 4 or 8ths, the odd cowbell. These were usually overdubs, so keep them loose.
- Low toms left to ring. Big, slow tom fills are part of the sound.

### Fills and changes
- Fills of 8ths or 16ths around the toms, often across the whole bar before a chorus.
- Triplet fills (three hits per beat) are a classic rock signature.
- Stop-time: the band hits only on the 1 for a few bars, then a fill brings the groove back.

### Sauce tips
- Quantize to 70 to 85%, or play the parts in by hand.
- Keep the overheads and room loud: the kit sound came mostly from them.
- Roll off some top end and add tape-style saturation.
- Nudge the snare a few milliseconds late for a laid-back blues feel.

@@ rock.hard
**The pocket.** Hard rock is the rock backbeat played louder, simpler and steadier. Phil Rudd of AC/DC is famous for a straight 8th groove that never wavers. In the 80s the drums got huge: big rooms, gated reverb on the snare, and toms that fill the speakers.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 110 to 140 BPM | 50% (straight) | Driving straight 8ths | Punchy kick that follows the riff |

### Kick
- Beat 1 and beat 3, plus the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 1 of the Arena groove. The Crunch kit uses the Rock groove, with its push on the & of 2 (step 7).
- In choruses, many players kick every quarter note or every 8th with the bass for a pounding drive.
- Keep every kick at nearly the same level.

### Snare and clap
- Backbeat on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), the loudest thing in the mix. Full velocity almost every time.
- For 80s hair metal, put a gated reverb on the snare: a big burst of room that cuts off short.

### Hats and cymbals
- 8th-note hats in verses, ride or crash 8ths in choruses. The Arena groove plays the ride.
- Crash on the 1 of each section and on the band's accents, doubled with a kick.
- A half-open hat gives the loud "wash" of a big rock record.

### Percussion and toms
- Cowbell on quarter notes for classic hard rock parts, tambourine doubling the snare.
- Big, low toms. A floor tom on 8ths instead of hats makes a heavy intro.

### Fills and changes
- The Arena groove's bar 2 is the stock fill: 16ths across the toms from step 11, high to floor, then a crash on the next 1.
- Band stabs: crash and kick together with the guitars, then silence.
- Half-time bridge: snare on beat 3 (step 9) only, so the groove feels half as fast and twice as heavy.

### Sauce tips
- Quantize kick and snare tightly (90 to 100%) and let the hats float a little.
- Layer a sample under the snare for 80s size, and keep the room mics loud.
- Gate the toms so they boom without ringing over everything.
- Raise the velocity over the last two beats before a chorus to push into it.

@@ rock.metal
**The pocket.** Metal drums are about power and precision. Double-kick runs, a hard snare and big cymbals lock with low, tight guitar riffs. The grid shows the classic double-kick beat: steady 16th-note kicks under a 2 and 4 snare and an 8th-note ride. Modern metal is often edited tight to the grid, while thrash and older metal keep more live push.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 to 200 BPM | 50% (nu metal can bounce at 54 to 58%) | 16th double kick, half-time breakdowns | Tight, clicky kick with the guitar chugs |

### Kick
- Double-kick 16ths (a double pedal or two kick drums, feet alternating), as in the grid. Keep the hits nearly level.
- Kicks often copy the guitar's palm-muted chugs exactly. Program the riff's rhythm into the kick.
- Metalcore and djent breakdowns follow syncopated chug patterns with gaps.

### Snare and clap
- Backbeat on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13) for the double-kick beat. Hard and consistent.
- Blast beat: kick and snare alternating (or together) on every 16th, with a cymbal riding along on top. Use it in short bursts.
- Thrash beat: kick on each beat and snare on each &, played fast.
- Half-time breakdown: snare on beat 3 (step 9) only.

### Hats and cymbals
- Ride on 8ths over the double kick, as in the grid, or on quarters. The ride's bell cuts through.
- China and crash cymbals on accents and breakdowns, crashes on quarter notes for big choruses.

### Percussion and toms
- No percussion beyond the odd cowbell in groove or nu metal.
- Low toms for fills and pounding tom sections with the kick.

### Fills and changes
- The grid's bar 2 ends with a fast fill: high tom then floor tom 16ths (steps 13 to 16).
- Breakdowns drop to half-time, often after a short silence.
- Stop for a bar before the last chorus, then return with a crash and double kick.

### Sauce tips
- Use triggered or sample-replaced kicks for clarity: short, clicky, every hit the same.
- Quantize hard (95 to 100%) for modern metal, looser for thrash and older heavy metal.
- At fast tempos, shorten the kick sample so the 16ths don't blur.
- For nu metal, try a hip-hop style kick pattern with a little swing under a heavy snare.

@@ rock.alt
**The pocket.** 90s alternative and grunge drums are heavy, loose and loud, with washy cymbals and a big room. Dave Grohl's playing with Nirvana is the template: simple parts hit very hard, with big fills. Post-punk and indie sit at the other end: dry, driving and repetitive, with steady hats.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 90 to 140 BPM | 50% (straight) | Straight 8ths, heavy backbeat | Roomy kick with the bass |

### Kick
- The Grunge groove: beat 1, the & of 2 (step 7) and beat 3, plus a kick on the a of 3 (step 12) in bar 2 that pushes into beat 4.
- The Room and Post-Punk kits use the Rock groove. Post-punk often goes to a kick on every beat, or to driving tom patterns.
- Kick and bass hit the riff's accents together.

### Snare and clap
- Backbeat on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), loud and roomy.
- Loud-quiet dynamics are the sound: a soft verse (or none), then the snare full force in the chorus.
- Indie and post-punk: a tight, dry snare, or rim clicks in quiet parts.

### Hats and cymbals
- The Grunge groove rides a half-open hat on 8ths at medium velocity. That sloshy wash is the sound. Riding a crash works too.
- The Grunge groove crashes on the 1 of each bar, a common loud-chorus move.
- Post-punk: tight closed hats, sometimes 16ths, very even.

### Percussion and toms
- Tambourine on 2 and 4 or on 8ths in the chorus.
- Floor tom 8ths instead of hats for a heavy verse, then switch to the crash for the chorus.

### Fills and changes
- Quiet verse with kick and closed hats, then a big snare fill and crash into a loud chorus.
- Grunge fills are often snare and tom 16ths, played loud and slightly rushed.
- Shoegaze and dream rock bury steady drums in reverb and rarely fill.

### Sauce tips
- Leave the timing loose: 75 to 85% quantize, and let the hats lag a little.
- Compress the room mics hard for the big, squashed grunge sound.
- Vary the open hat velocity so it breathes instead of sounding like one long noise.
- For post-punk, keep it dry: a short, bright snare and a steady hat.

@@ rock.punk
**The pocket.** Punk is fast, tight and relentless. The signature is the skate beat: kick on every beat and snare on every & (the off-beats), so the beat bounces like a fast polka. Pop punk (Blink-182, Green Day) and emo mix that with driving straight-8th rock beats and big fills. Energy matters more than subtlety.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 150 to 200 BPM | 50% (straight) | Fast 8ths, skate beat | Short kick, bass on 8ths |

### Kick
- In the grid: every beat (steps 1, 5, 9, 13), with an extra kick on the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 2 that doubles the snare.
- In the driving rock beat, the kick plays 1 and 3 plus 8th-note pushes that match the bass guitar.
- Keep it short and punchy so fast notes stay clear.

### Snare and clap
- Skate beat: snare on every & (steps 3, 7, 11, 15). Strong and even.
- Driving beat: back to 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), a usual verse choice in pop punk.
- Snare rolls and snare-heavy fills lead into choruses.

### Hats and cymbals
- Closed hats on 8ths in the grid. In loud sections, ride a crash or an open hat on 8ths or quarters.
- A crash on the 1 of every bar, as in the grid, is a common chorus move. Alternate two crashes, left and right.

### Percussion and toms
- Very little. At most a tambourine on 8ths in a pop punk chorus.
- Toms for fills, and floor tom 8ths for a tom-groove intro.

### Fills and changes
- A common pop punk plan: driving 8ths in the verse, skate beat in the chorus, half-time bridge (snare on beat 3, step 9).
- Fast 16th snare and tom fills into choruses, ending with a crash.
- Stops: the band hits the 1 together and rests, then the drums count back in.

### Sauce tips
- Quantize tightly but not to 100%. Keep a few milliseconds of life.
- Vary the skate-beat snares a little. At this speed a real drummer can't hit every one at full force.
- Use a bright, cracking snare and a short kick. Long samples smear at this tempo.
- Push the overheads up in the chorus. Punk choruses are cymbal-heavy.

@@ country
**The pocket.** Country drums serve the song and the singer. The classic feel is the two-step, or "boom-chick": kick on 1 and 3 with the bass alternating root and fifth (the boom), and snare or cross-stick on 2 and 4 (the chick). Parts stay simple, the dynamics follow the lyric, and brushes, rim clicks and shakers do a lot of the work.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 80 to 130 BPM | 50 to 54% (shuffles 60 to 66%) | Two-step, straight or shuffled 8ths | Kick with the bass on 1 and 3 |

### Kick
- Beats 1 and 3 (steps 1 and 9), as in the Country and Shuffle grooves. That's the "boom" of boom-chick.
- Bigger country (the Arena groove) adds rock pushes like the & of 3 (step 11).
- The Country Trap kit uses the Bounce groove at trap tempo (150 BPM, clap on beat 3), with an 808 under a trap kick.

### Snare and clap
- Cross-stick (the stick laid across the snare so it clicks on the rim) on 2 and 4 in verses, as in the Country groove.
- Full snare for the chorus. The Country groove shows the pickup: snares on beat 4 and the & of 4 (steps 13 and 15) at the end of bar 2.
- Train beat: snare 16ths with accents on 2 and 4, often with brushes.

### Hats and cymbals
- Light 8th-note hats. In a shuffle, swing the 8ths toward a triplet feel: the Shuffle groove plays them at 62%.
- Ride or open hats in the chorus, crash on the 1 of each section.

### Percussion and toms
- Tambourine on 2 and 4 and shaker 16ths are close to standard on Nashville records (the Country groove's Perc 1 and Perc 2 lanes).
- Woodblock or cowbell on the beats for honky tonk flavor.
- Toms for short fills and floor-tom builds.

### Fills and changes
- Short fills: a beat or two of snare or toms into the chorus. Country drummers rarely overplay.
- Verse on cross-stick and shaker, chorus on snare and ride, bridge pulled back.

### Sauce tips
- Keep velocity low in verses and save the big hits for the chorus.
- Humanize the shaker: alternate loud and soft 16ths and let the timing wander slightly.
- Keep brushes and cross-stick dry and close.
- Pick a style above for honky tonk and train beats, modern Nashville, country rap or Americana.

@@ country.classic
**The pocket.** Classic country, honky tonk and outlaw records (Buck Owens, Merle Haggard, Willie Nelson, Johnny Cash) use small, dry kits that stay out of the singer's way. The two main feels are the shuffle two-step, where the 8ths swing toward a triplet, and the train beat, a steady chug of snare 16ths like a train on the tracks. Brushes and a woody snare are the sound.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 96 to 140 BPM | 60 to 66% shuffle, 50% (straight) train beat | Shuffle two-step or train beat | Light kick with the bass on 1 and 3 |

### Kick
- Beats 1 and 3 (steps 1 and 9) in both grooves. Light and warm, more felt than heard.
- Follow the bass's root notes and leave room for its walk-ups.

### Snare and clap
- Shuffle: snare on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), often brushes or cross-stick.
- Train beat: snare on every 16th, as in the Train Beat groove. Accent beats 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), play the &s medium and ghost the rest.
- With brushes, one hand sweeps in a circle for a steady "shh" while the other taps the accents.

### Hats and cymbals
- Hats on 8ths in the Shuffle groove. Swing them by moving every & (steps 3, 7, 11, 15) late toward a triplet, as the groove does at 62%. If your swing setting has a note value, set it to 8ths.
- Western swing (Bob Wills) leans on brushes and a light ride, like a jazz drummer.
- Crashes are rare and soft.

### Percussion and toms
- Woodblock or shaker on 8ths with accents on the beats, as in the Shuffle groove's Perc 1 lane. Its Perc 2 lane marks beats 1 and 3.
- In the Train Beat groove, Perc 1 adds shaker-style 16ths.

### Fills and changes
- Fills are short: a snare drag or two beats of 8ths into the chorus.
- Drop to brushes or cross-stick under a steel guitar or fiddle solo.
- Many songs keep one feel throughout. Build with stronger accents, not more notes.

### Sauce tips
- The grid's levels make a good guide: about 56 velocity for ghosts, 91 for medium hits, 124 for accents.
- Use a dry, close snare and keep the kit narrow for a vintage, near-mono feel.
- Quantize loosely (70 to 85%) and vary the velocity of every hit.
- For outlaw grit, hit the snare harder and add tape saturation.

@@ country.modern
**The pocket.** Modern Nashville mixes a live kit with programmed loops. Real drums play a pop-rock backbeat, with a loop, snaps or a drum machine underneath, especially in verses. Since the 2010s, bro country and country pop (Florida Georgia Line, Sam Hunt) have borrowed pop and hip-hop beats, but the chorus usually still lands on a big live kit.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 75 to 130 BPM | 50% (straight) | Straight 8ths and 16ths | Live kick, sometimes an electronic layer |

### Kick
- Radio and Snap kits use the Country groove: beats 1 and 3, like a two-step. The Country Pop kit's Pop groove adds the & of 2 (step 7); the Stadium kit's Arena groove adds the & of 3 (step 11).
- Layer the live kick with a tight electronic kick for weight.

### Snare and clap
- Cross-stick on 2 and 4 in verses, as in the Country groove, and a full snare in the chorus.
- Snaps or claps layered with the snare are a modern signature; the Pop groove stacks clap and snare.
- The Country groove's snare pickup (steps 13 and 15 of bar 2) leads into the next section.

### Hats and cymbals
- Live 8th-note hats, often with programmed 16th hats or a shaker loop on top.
- Ride or open-hat wash in the chorus, crash on the 1 of each section.

### Percussion and toms
- Tambourine on 2 and 4 and shaker 16ths (accent the 8ths), as in the Country groove's Perc 1 and Perc 2 lanes.
- Floor-tom builds into the last chorus. Stadium kits use big tom fills like the Arena groove's bar 2.

### Fills and changes
- A typical build: loop and snaps in verse 1, live kit on cross-stick in verse 2, full kit and ride in the chorus.
- Strip back to the loop for the bridge, then bring the kit back on a crash.
- A reverse cymbal or a short snare build pushes into the final chorus.

### Sauce tips
- Keep the loop tight to the grid and let the live kit sit a little loose against it.
- Cut the lows from the loop so it doesn't clash with the live kick.
- Vary shaker velocity and nudge it slightly late for a relaxed feel.
- Give the chorus snare a short, bright plate reverb.

@@ country.rap
**The pocket.** Country rap puts trap drums under country songs: an 808, a half-time clap and rolling hats, with twangy guitar, banjo or a live tambourine on top. Lil Nas X's "Old Town Road" took the sound mainstream. Keep the trap bounce and let a few live sounds into the kit.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 130 to 160 BPM | 50% (Boom Bap groove 60%) | Half-time, 8th hats with rolls | 808 with the kick |

### Kick
- Kick and 808 together: beat 1, then syncopated hits like the & of 3 (step 11) or the & of 4 (step 15), as in the Bounce groove. The Truck Bounce kit's Trap groove adds the a of 2 (step 8).
- Leave space around the beat-3 clap.
- The Backroad kit's Boom Bap groove is slower and swings hard (90 BPM, 60% swing), with no 808: kick on 1, 3 and the & of 3, snare on 2 and 4.

### Snare and clap
- Clap (or clap and snare) on beat 3 (step 9): the half-time backbeat. At 140 BPM it feels like 70.
- Layer a live snare or a stomp under the clap for a country edge.
- A snap on beat 2 (step 5, the Bounce groove's Perc 1 lane) fills the gap.

### Hats and cymbals
- 8th hats with 32nd-note doubles and 16th-note triplet rolls, like steps 10 and 16 in the Bounce groove's bar 2.
- An open hat on the & of 2 (step 7) for a lift.

### Percussion and toms
- A tambourine or shaker played in by hand brings the live side.
- Perc hits on the a of 2 and the a of 4 (steps 8 and 16, the Bounce groove's Perc 2 lane) add bounce.

### 808 and bass
- Tune the 808 to the song's key and follow the guitar's root notes.
- Short 808s under a busy guitar or banjo, long gliding ones in sparse parts.

### Fills and changes
- Drop the 808 and drums for the hook's first line, then bring them back in full.
- Hat rolls and a reverse cymbal into the hook, not tom fills.
- For a rockier hook, switch to a live snare on 2 and 4.

### Sauce tips
- Vary hat velocity: accent the beats and soften the rolls.
- Keep tambourine and stomps a little loose against the tight programmed drums.
- Pick a clap with some room tone to blend with acoustic guitars.

@@ country.americana
**The pocket.** Americana and folk drums sound like a band in a room, or on a porch. The best-known modern sound is stomp and clap: a kick on every beat (four on the floor), claps on 2 and 4 and a tambourine, heard on records by The Lumineers and Mumford & Sons. Roots rock and alt country use a plain kit with brushes, cross-stick and space.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 70 to 130 BPM | 50 to 54% (the Shuffle groove 62%) | Four-on-the-floor stomp or two-step | Big, soft kick or a floor stomp |

### Kick
- The Stomp & Clap groove: kick on every beat (steps 1, 5, 9, 13). Use a big, soft, low kick, or a foot stomp on a wooden floor.
- Front Porch kit (Shuffle groove): kick on 1 and 3, very light, under 8ths swung at 62%. The Folk Room kit's Worship groove is a slow 72 BPM and adds the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 1.
- Roots Rock uses the Rock groove, with a push on the & of 2 (step 7).

### Snare and clap
- Claps on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13), stacked from several takes to sound like a group.
- Cross-stick or brushes on 2 and 4 for quiet songs.
- A full snare only in the biggest choruses, if at all.

### Hats and cymbals
- Many folk tracks have no hats, like the Stomp & Clap groove: its Perc 1 lane (tambourine) doubles the claps and Perc 2 (shaker) plays 8ths.
- Where there are cymbals, a dark ride or a soft crash on the 1 of the chorus.

### Percussion and toms
- Tambourine on 2 and 4 or on 8ths, shaker 8ths, woodblock or a stomp box: hand percussion is the backbone.
- Floor tom 8ths with mallets build energy.

### Fills and changes
- Start with guitar and a stomp, add claps in the chorus, then stomp, clap and tambourine together, with an extra stomp layer, for the last chorus.
- Fills are rare. A floor-tom roll or a single crash is enough.

### Sauce tips
- Layer claps from several takes, each slightly off, panned wide.
- Leave stomps and claps loose: 60 to 80% quantize, or none.
- Put everything in one natural room reverb so it sounds like one space.
- Vary tambourine velocity on every hit, with accents on the beat.

@@ worship
**The pocket.** Worship drums are about the build. A song often starts with almost nothing, grows through cross-stick and toms, peaks with the full kit in the last chorus, then comes back down. Parts stay simple and the dynamics do the talking. Gospel adds chops and ghost notes; ambient worship strips it back to swells and soft pulses.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 66 to 80 BPM (upbeat songs 100 to 140) | 50% (Gospel Chops 55%) | 8ths and 16ths, long builds | Kick with bass and synth pads |

### Kick
- Beats 1 and 3 with a push on the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 1, as in the Worship groove.
- Big choruses often go four on the floor (a kick on every beat) for drive.
- The Gospel Chops kit plays busy kicks with an 808, at 100 BPM with a light 55% swing.

### Snare and clap
- Cross-stick on 2 and 4 in verses, full snare in the chorus (steps 5 and 13).
- Modern worship layers a clap with the snare, as in the Big Room groove.
- A half-time bridge (snare on beat 3, step 9) feels bigger without speeding up.

### Hats and cymbals
- 8th-note hats in the Worship groove. The Big Room groove plays closed hats on the beat and open hats on the &s.
- Ride in the chorus, and cymbal swells (soft mallet rolls that grow louder) into new sections, landing on a crash.

### Percussion and toms
- Tambourine on 2 and 4 and a shaker on 8ths, as in the Worship groove's Perc 1 and Perc 2 lanes.
- Floor-tom builds: steady 8ths or quarters with mallets, louder over 4 to 8 bars.

### Fills and changes
- The usual arc: pads, then cross-stick or toms in the verse, full kit in the chorus, a long bridge build, a huge last chorus, a quiet ending.
- A build: floor tom 8ths, then 16ths, then snare 16ths, then a crash.
- Drop out for a bar before the biggest chorus.

### Sauce tips
- Draw velocity ramps for builds, each bar a little louder than the last.
- Keep the tempo steady. Most modern worship teams play to a click and backing tracks.
- Use a big hall or plate reverb, especially on toms.
- Pick a style above for modern worship, gospel chops or ambient worship.

@@ worship.modern
**The pocket.** Modern worship (Hillsong, Bethel, Elevation Worship) sounds like a big pop-rock band with pads and loops. Many songs are slow, around 70 BPM, felt in 8ths and 16ths, and climb from cross-stick or toms to a full kit in the last chorus. Loops and claps often run under the live kit.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 66 to 80 BPM (upbeat songs 100 to 140) | 50% (straight) | 8ths and 16ths, long builds | Kick with bass and synth bass |

### Kick
- The Worship groove: beats 1 and 3, plus the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 1.
- Anthem choruses often go four on the floor (a kick on every beat).
- The Electronic kit layers a programmed kick with the live one.

### Snare and clap
- Cross-stick on 2 and 4 for verses, full snare for choruses.
- The Big Room groove (104 BPM, for the Anthem and Electronic kits): snare and clap together on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13).
- Half-time chorus or bridge: snare on beat 3 (step 9) only. Big and slow.

### Hats and cymbals
- The Worship groove plays 8th hats; the Big Room groove plays closed hats on the beat and open hats on the &s.
- Ride, or the crash, on 8ths in the final chorus.
- Cymbal swells: a soft mallet roll that grows over a bar into a new section.

### Percussion and toms
- Tambourine on 2 and 4 and shaker 8ths. The Acoustic kit leans on cajon and shaker.
- Floor tom 8ths or quarters, often with mallets, carry the build before the snare enters.

### Fills and changes
- The Big Room groove's bar 2 ends with a quick fill: high tom, mid tom, two floor-tom hits (steps 13 to 16).
- Bridges often repeat and build for 8 to 16 bars: toms, snare 8ths, snare 16ths, then a huge chorus.
- Pull everything out for a quiet tag at the end.

### Sauce tips
- Draw a velocity ramp across each build: the last bar clearly louder than the first.
- Keep the loop tight and the live kit slightly loose on top.
- Long hall reverb on toms and swells, a shorter plate on the snare.
- A reverse cymbal into the last chorus stands in for a live swell.

@@ worship.gospel
**The pocket.** Gospel drumming is where church music meets funk and R&B: a deep backbeat with ghost notes, busy syncopated kicks and fast, flashy fills called "chops" between phrases. Drummers follow the singer and organ closely, so the band can lift or stop in a moment. Modern and trap gospel add 808s and claps.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 70 to 130 BPM (praise breaks faster) | 50 to 58% (Gospel Chops 55%) | Lightly swung 16ths, ghost notes | Kick with an 808 or synth bass |

### Kick
- Syncopated, as in the Gospel Chops groove: beat 1, the a of 1 (step 4), the & of 2 (step 7) and the & of 3 (step 11).
- Shout music (a praise break) jumps to a much faster, driving dance beat.

### Snare and clap
- Backbeat on 2 and 4 (steps 5 and 13) with a clap layered on top.
- Ghost notes (very soft snare taps) on the e and a of each beat are essential. Add them yourself.
- Bar 2 adds snares on the a of 3 (step 12) and the & of 4 (step 15), a classic pickup.

### Hats and cymbals
- The hats gallop: each beat, its & and its a ("1 & a, 2 & a"), as in the grid.
- Crash on the 1 after a fill, often choked (grabbed to cut it short).

### Percussion and toms
- Tambourine on 2 and 4 or 16ths.
- Toms for chops, tuned higher and punchy.

### 808 and bass
- The 808 doubles most kicks; leave a few without it for punch.
- Keep 808s short so fast kicks stay clear. The Choir Hall kit drops the 808; Trap Gospel uses the Bounce groove at trap tempo (150 BPM, clap on beat 3).

### Fills and changes
- Chops: fast 16th and 32nd runs around snare and toms, often in groups of six (sextuplets), ending on a crash.
- Follow the song leader: hits, stops and vamps (a repeated section) that build each time.
- Mark a key change with a big fill and a crash.

### Sauce tips
- Ghost notes around 30 to 55 velocity, backbeats near the top.
- The Gospel Chops groove swings its 16ths at 55%. Go up to 58% for a looser feel, and nudge the backbeat slightly late.
- Use a tight, bright, high-tuned snare.
- Program chops at full speed, then remove a few notes so they breathe.

@@ worship.ambient
**The pocket.** Ambient worship, soaking and prayer music barely use drums. When they do, the drums are swells, soft pulses and distant toms that sit inside long pads. Hymns played this way keep a gentle, natural kit. The goal is space and calm, so every hit needs room to ring.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 60 to 75 BPM, or free time | 50% (straight) | Slow, sparse pulse | Soft kick or a mallet floor tom |

### Kick
- The Worship groove shows the full version: beats 1 and 3, plus the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 1. In ambient parts, use only beat 1, or 1 and 3, very soft.
- A soft, felt-beater kick or a low tom works better than a punchy kick.
- A gentle pulse, one soft kick on every beat, keeps time under a long pad.

### Snare and clap
- Cross-stick or brushes on 2 and 4, very quiet. Many songs leave the snare out completely.
- When a section opens up, try a soft half-time snare on beat 3 (step 9) with a long reverb.

### Hats and cymbals
- The grid's 8th hats are for the fullest parts. Elsewhere hats drop out, and a triangle or a shaker on 8ths at low velocity takes their place.
- Cymbal swells with mallets are the main sound: start silent, grow over a bar or two, then fade.
- A soft ride bell or a reverse cymbal adds movement.

### Percussion and toms
- Floor tom and low toms with mallets for slow, rolling pulses.
- Triangle, shaker or a soft tambourine on 2 and 4, one percussion part at a time.

### Fills and changes
- No real fills. Changes come from swells and from adding or removing layers.
- Build from swells, to a mallet tom pulse, to a soft full kit in the last chorus, then fade back to swells.
- Leave whole sections with no drums at all.

### Sauce tips
- Use long reverb (hall or shimmer) and let it become part of the drum sound.
- Draw velocity ramps for swells, or a volume ramp on a long cymbal sample.
- Keep everything soft, most hits around 40 to 80 velocity.
- For the Hymn Room kit, use a warm, natural kit in a real room, with brushes or light sticks.

@@ cinematic
**The pocket.** Cinematic drums follow the picture and the story, not a dance floor. The language is big low hits, ensemble toms (many drums played as one), ostinatos (short repeating rhythms) and impacts on the big moments. The grid shows the core: a low-tom ostinato, a snare on beat 3, booms on the downbeats, and an impact with a crash on the 1.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 80 to 140 BPM | 50% (straight) | Half-time weight over a driving ostinato | Big low booms, sub hits |

### Kick
- A big low boom on beats 1 and 3 (steps 1 and 9), with an extra on the & of 2 (step 7) in bar 2.
- Use huge, roomy kicks or an orchestral bass drum, not a dry pop kick.
- The Hybrid kit puts an 808 or sub boom under the hits.

### Snare and clap
- One snare on beat 3 (step 9): a half-time backbeat that feels huge.
- Layer orchestral snares, a clap with a long tail or a metallic hit.
- Snare rolls (fast 16ths or 32nds) build tension before a hit.

### Hats and cymbals
- Few hats. Shakers, ticking percussion or metal hits on 8ths or 16ths give motion.
- Crash with the impact on the 1. Cymbal rolls and reverse cymbals lead into it.

### Percussion and toms
- Low toms play the ostinato: on every beat plus the & of 3 (step 11), turning into 16ths at the end of bar 2.
- Mid toms double the low toms on accents: the & of 3 (step 11) in bar 1, steps 13 and 15 in bar 2.
- A percussion part on every beat holds the pulse: taiko, frame drums, congas or woodblock.

### Fills and changes
- Risers (sounds that climb in pitch or volume) lead into impacts on the 1 of a new section.
- Use silence: cut everything for a beat or two before a big hit.
- Grow the ostinato, adding a layer every 4 or 8 bars.

### Sauce tips
- Layer impacts: a low boom, a tom and a crash together make one big hit.
- Vary tom velocity in the ostinato and accent the first note of each group.
- Put toms in a big hall and pan the ensemble wide.
- Pick a style above for epic trailers, horror or world percussion.

@@ cinematic.trailer
**The pocket.** Trailer music comes in waves: quiet tension, a rise, a huge hit, then a bigger section. The drums sit under brass blasts called "braams" (made famous by trailers like the one for Inception), with taiko and ensemble toms pounding ostinatos and huge impacts on every cut. Everything is loud, wide and low.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 90 to 140 BPM | 50% (straight) | Driving ostinato, half-time hits | Sub booms and huge low impacts |

### Kick
- Low booms on beats 1 and 3 (steps 1 and 9), with a push on the & of 2 (step 7) in bar 2.
- Orchestral bass drums or processed booms with a long tail.
- Layer a sub hit under the first boom of every section.

### Snare and clap
- Half-time snare on beat 3 (step 9), heavy and roomy.
- Stack snares, claps and metal hits into one big crack.
- Snare rolls and 16th-note builds lead into the drop.

### Hats and cymbals
- Few hats. Ticking clocks, metal pings or shakers on 16ths add tension.
- Cymbal swells and reverse cymbals lead into every impact. Crash with the impact on the 1.

### Percussion and toms
- Taiko and ensemble toms play the ostinato: low toms on the beats and the & of 3 (step 11), mid toms doubling the accents.
- Hero sections drive harder, with 8th or 16th-note toms under the melody.
- The perc part on every beat holds the pulse.

### Fills and changes
- The classic shape: tension, rise, hit, bigger section, rise, bigger hit, a final short hit (a "stinger").
- The grid's bar 2 ends with low-tom 16ths and a second effect on beat 4 (step 13), leading into the next impact.
- Stop completely for half a bar before the biggest hit.

### Sauce tips
- Stack each impact: boom, low tom, braam, crash, all on the same 1.
- Duck the music for a moment when an impact lands so it punches through.
- Use a big hall reverb, but cut the lows out of the reverb so the booms stay clear.
- Accent the first hit of each tom group and soften the rest.

@@ cinematic.horror
**The pocket.** Horror drums are about tension, not groove. Long silences, low booms, a slow heartbeat pulse and sudden hits (jump scares) do the work. Sounds are often reversed, pitched down, metallic or scraped. The grid shows the full-power version; a horror cue usually uses only part of it.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 60 to 100 BPM, or no pulse | 50% (straight) | Slow, uneven, tense | Deep booms and sub drones |

### Kick
- A heartbeat: two low hits close together, like beat 1 and the e or & after it (steps 1 and 2, or 1 and 3), then a pause. Repeat it every beat or every bar.
- The grid's kick on beats 1 and 3 works as a slow, heavy pulse.
- Deep, soft kicks or a low tom pitched down.

### Snare and clap
- Rarely a backbeat. Use the snare on beat 3 (step 9) only for a heavy, industrial pulse.
- Reversed snares and claps that swell into a hit.
- Snare rolls that grow from silence to a sudden stop.

### Hats and cymbals
- Bowed or scraped cymbals, metal scrapes and reverse cymbals instead of hats.
- A ticking clock sound or a triangle on 8ths for nervous tension.

### Percussion and toms
- Low toms and booms, spaced unevenly so the listener can't predict them.
- Woodblock, metal and found sounds (pipes, sheet metal, doors) for industrial horror.
- The grid's low-tom ostinato works for a chase scene.

### Fills and changes
- Build with a riser or swell, cut to silence, then hit.
- Slow the pulse down, or let it stop, before a scare.
- For the Whisper kit, keep to soft, reversed hits and quiet pulses.

### Sauce tips
- Pitch drum samples down several semitones for weight and unease.
- Use reverse reverb so hits swell in out of nowhere.
- Leave uneven gaps between hits. Exact repetition starts to feel safe.
- Muffle most of the kit with a low-pass filter and let only the impacts be bright.

@@ cinematic.world
**The pocket.** World percussion in film and game scores borrows from real traditions: Japanese taiko ensembles, Middle Eastern frame drums and darbuka, West African djembe and Latin congas. The feel comes from hand drums playing interlocking parts, each simple on its own, adding up to a rolling groove. Program them like an ensemble, not a drum machine.

| Tempo | Swing | Time feel | Low end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 80 to 130 BPM | 50% (players add a little by feel) | Interlocking hand drums, steady pulse | Taiko or a big frame drum |

### Kick
- Low taiko or a big frame drum on beats 1 and 3 (steps 1 and 9), with a push on the & of 2 (step 7) in bar 2.
- In frame-drum and darbuka patterns, the low tone (the "dum") works as the kick.

### Snare and clap
- The grid's snare on beat 3 (step 9) can be a high taiko hit, a frame-drum accent or a group clap.
- Frame drums and darbuka use a high, sharp "tek" in place of a snare.

### Hats and cymbals
- No hats. Shakers, rattles and bells give the 8th and 16th-note motion.
- Finger cymbals or a gong for accents and section starts.

### Percussion and toms
- Taiko: the grid's low-tom ostinato, played as an ensemble. Players also click the rim ("ka") between the big center hits ("don").
- Frame drums: the Middle Eastern maqsum rhythm, "dum tek - tek dum - tek -" in 8ths. Low on 1 (step 1), high on the & of 1 (step 3) and the & of 2 (step 7), low on 3 (step 9), high on 4 (step 13).
- Congas, djembe and woodblock parts that answer each other.

### Fills and changes
- Rolls on taiko or frame drum before a new section, ending in a hit where every drum plays together.
- Add instruments one at a time: shaker, then frame drum, then taiko.
- The Tribal Pulse kit leans on toms for a driving build.

### Sauce tips
- Use several takes or samples of each drum, spread across the stereo field.
- Keep the timing human. Never quantize hand drums to 100%.
- Vary velocity a lot: hand drums mix strong accents with soft filler strokes.
- Use a natural room reverb so the drums sound like one ensemble.

@@ era.0
**The 60s sound.** Drums were played by a drummer in a real room, usually at the same time as the band, and captured with only a few microphones, sometimes just one overhead and one on the kick. Tape machines had only a few tracks, so the whole kit was often squeezed onto one track and sat behind the vocals. Kicks were round and boomy, snares rang, cymbals were dark and washy, and many hit records were mixed in mono.

### Gear and sounds
- American kits from Ludwig, Gretsch, Rogers and Slingerland; Ringo Starr's Ludwig with the Beatles is the famous one.
- A tambourine doubling the backbeat (a Motown trademark), plus hand claps, shakers, congas and bongos.
- Echo chambers, plate reverb and tape slapback echo for space.
- Brushes, ride cymbal patterns and shuffles carried over from jazz and early rock and roll.

### Get it in your DAW
- Pick warm, roomy sounds: a whole-kit or overhead-style sample often feels more 60s than tight, close-miked hits.
- Roll off the top end (a low-pass around 8 to 10 kHz) and the deep sub, then add tape saturation.
- Play it like a person: vary the velocity on every hit, nudge some notes a few milliseconds off the grid, and avoid copy-pasting identical bars.
- Keep the drums narrow or even mono, and lower in the mix than you would today.
- Avoid sub-heavy kicks, clicky snares, bright crisp hats and hard limiting.

@@ era.0.hiphop
**Hip-hop in the 60s.** Hip-hop didn't exist yet; it was born in the Bronx in the 1970s. Picking the 60s gives your kit warm, roomy, live-played tones while the patterns stay modern, so treat it as a vintage flip: a boom bap groove on a dusty 60s kit sounds like a beat built from an old soul record.

@@ era.0.trap
**Trap in the 60s.** Trap didn't exist until the 2000s. Picking the 60s keeps the modern trap patterns but gives the kit warm, roomy, live-sounding drums, so try a vintage flip: a soft, round kick and a ringing snare over modern hat rolls, with the 808 kept low and warm underneath.

@@ era.0.rnb
**R&B in the 60s.** This was the decade of Motown and soul: live drummers, a tambourine doubling the backbeat, hand claps and a roomy kit sitting behind the vocals. By the end of the decade James Brown's records were pushing the beat toward funk, with tight snares and syncopated kicks.

@@ era.0.pop
**Pop in the 60s.** Pop drums were live and roomy, played by session drummers and band members alike. The "Wall of Sound" records bathed the kit in echo, Hal Blaine's intro to "Be My Baby" became one of the most famous beats ever, and the Beatles with Ringo Starr set the pop-rock template.

@@ era.0.rock
**Rock in the 60s.** Rock drums were live, loose and roomy, often recorded with a few mics onto a single track. Ringo Starr and Charlie Watts played swinging, in-the-pocket parts, Keith Moon played wild, fill-heavy ones, and by the end of the decade heavier bands were turning the kit up and letting the toms boom.

@@ era.0.country
**Country in the 60s.** The Nashville sound kept the drums soft and low, often brushes on the snare with a light kick, while Bakersfield acts like Buck Owens and Merle Haggard used brighter, driving backbeats. The train beat, a steady, chugging snare pattern with accents on 2 and 4, kept many songs rolling.

@@ era.0.worship
**Worship in the 60s.** Modern worship bands didn't exist yet: most church music was hymns with organ and piano, while gospel had Hammond organ, hand claps and, in many churches, a live drummer. Picking the 60s gives your kit warm, roomy, live tones, which suits a hymn-room or gospel-choir feel with the modern patterns on top.

@@ era.0.edm
**EDM in the 60s.** Electronic dance music didn't exist yet; drum machines only reached the dance floor years later. Picking the 60s keeps the modern dance patterns but swaps in warm, roomy, live tones, so try a vintage flip: a four on the floor groove on a 60s kit with tambourine on the offbeats, like a soul record built for the club.

@@ era.0.lofi
**Lo-fi in the 60s.** Lo-fi hip-hop didn't exist yet, but 60s soul and jazz records are exactly what lo-fi producers love to sample. Picking the 60s gives the kit warm, roomy, live tones under the modern patterns, which suits a jazzy, brushy beat that sounds like a record you found in a crate.

@@ era.0.cinematic
**Cinematic drums in the 60s.** Film scores used orchestral percussion: timpani, snare drum, bass drum and cymbals, plus jazz kits in spy and detective themes. Ennio Morricone's western scores added whip cracks, bells and unusual percussion. Picking the 60s gives roomy, warm, live tones while the patterns stay modern.

@@ era.1
**The 70s sound.** Studios moved to 16 and 24-track tape, so each drum could get its own close mic, and the dry, deadened sound took over: kicks with the front head off or a pillow inside, towels or tape on snares and toms, and small, dead rooms. Funk and soul drummers played tight, busy 16th-note hats with ghost notes (very soft snare hits between the main ones), and disco made the four on the floor kick (a kick on every beat) a dance-floor standard. Hard rock went the other way, with drums recorded in big live rooms, like John Bonham's with Led Zeppelin.

### Gear and sounds
- Kits with concert toms (one head, no bottom head) and metal snares like the Ludwig Supraphonic.
- Congas, bongos, cowbell and timbales in funk, soul, disco and Latin-flavored records.
- The Syndrum and other early electronic drums gave late-70s disco its "pew" tom sound.
- Plate reverb, tape and hard-working compressors on the drums.

### Get it in your DAW
- Choose dry, muffled kicks and snares, and toms with a low thud rather than a long ring.
- Use little reverb: a short room or plate, kept low.
- Warm it up with tape saturation and a gentle low-pass, and tame harsh 3 to 5 kHz.
- For funk, play busy 16th hats with accents, ghost notes and light swing (about 54 to 58%); for disco, keep a steady four on the floor with open hats on the offbeats.
- Avoid gated reverb, long 808 subs and bright modern hats.

@@ era.1.hiphop
**Hip-hop in the 70s.** Hip-hop was born at Bronx block parties, where DJs like Kool Herc stretched out the drum breaks of funk records, switching between two copies, to keep dancers going. The first rap records at the end of the decade, like "Rapper's Delight", used live studio bands, so the drums were dry, tight 70s funk and disco.

@@ era.1.trap
**Trap in the 70s.** Trap didn't exist yet; it came out of Atlanta in the 2000s. Picking the 70s keeps the modern trap patterns but gives the kit dry, deadened, warm tones, so try a vintage flip: a muffled funk kick and snare with a soft 808 underneath, like a trap beat built on a 70s soul sample.

@@ era.1.rnb
**R&B in the 70s.** Funk and soul ruled: dry, tight kits, busy 16th-note hats, ghost notes on the snare and syncopated kicks. Clyde Stubblefield's "Funky Drummer" break with James Brown, the smooth grooves of Philly soul and the four on the floor of disco all came out of this decade's R&B.

@@ era.1.pop
**Pop in the 70s.** Pop drums went dry and close-miked: soft rock had muffled, deadened kits, and disco brought the steady four on the floor kick with open hats on the offbeats. ABBA, the Bee Gees and Fleetwood Mac all had warm, tight, in-the-pocket drums.

@@ era.1.rock
**Rock in the 70s.** Hard rock grew huge, with John Bonham's big, roomy drums on Led Zeppelin records the best-known example, while soft rock and studio rock went dry and deadened. From the mid-70s, punk bands like the Ramones played fast, straight 8th-note beats with no frills.

@@ era.1.country
**Country in the 70s.** Outlaw country from Willie Nelson and Waylon Jennings brought looser, rock-influenced live drums, while the smoother countrypolitan sound kept the kit soft under strings. Tones were dry and warm, with shuffles, two-step beats and train beats all common.

@@ era.1.worship
**Worship in the 70s.** Modern worship bands didn't exist yet, but the Jesus Movement was starting to bring guitars and drums into church songs, and gospel had big choirs over live drummers and Hammond organ. Picking the 70s gives the kit dry, warm, deadened tones, a good fit for a soft acoustic or gospel feel with modern patterns.

@@ era.1.edm
**EDM in the 70s.** EDM didn't exist yet, but disco is its parent: a four on the floor kick (one on every beat), open hats on the offbeats and claps on 2 and 4. Giorgio Moroder's synth disco with Donna Summer pointed the way. Picking the 70s gives warm, dry, live tones for a disco or nu-disco flip.

@@ era.1.lofi
**Lo-fi in the 70s.** Lo-fi hip-hop didn't exist yet, but 70s jazz, soul and funk records are its favorite sample sources. Picking the 70s gives the kit dry, deadened, warm tones under the modern patterns, so try a vintage flip: a muffled kick, a soft snare and quiet 16th hats with plenty of swing.

@@ era.1.cinematic
**Cinematic drums in the 70s.** Big orchestral scores came back with John Williams' "Jaws" and "Star Wars", and the drums were the orchestra's: timpani, snare drum, bass drum and cymbals. Action and detective films also used funk, like the 16th-note hi-hat that drives Isaac Hayes' "Theme from Shaft". Expect dry, warm tones.

@@ era.2
**The 80s sound.** Drum machines and digital samples arrived on hit records, and snares got huge. The LinnDrum and Oberheim DMX played sampled drums on pop and funk records, the Roland TR-808 and TR-909 powered electro, early hip-hop and dance music, and gated reverb (a big room or reverb cut off sharply by a gate) made snares and toms sound massive but short. Live drums were recorded in big rooms, compressed hard and soaked in bright digital reverb.

### Gear and sounds
- Roland TR-808 (booming kick, snappy snare, handclap, cowbell) and TR-909 (punchy kick, crisp hats).
- Linn machines (the LM-1, then the LinnDrum) and the Oberheim DMX: tight sampled kits heard on records by Prince and many others.
- Simmons electronic drums, with their hexagonal pads and synthetic "zap" toms.
- Gated reverb on snares and toms, made famous by Phil Collins' "In the Air Tonight".

### Get it in your DAW
- Gate the snare reverb: send the snare to a big room or plate, then gate the reverb so it stops after a few hundred milliseconds.
- Pick drum machine sounds (808, 909, Linn, DMX) or big, bright live snares, and layer a clap on the backbeat.
- Quantize machine parts hard; save the swing for funk, early hip-hop and new jack swing.
- Write big tom fills and hit a crash on the downbeat of each new section.
- Avoid gliding 808 basslines, trap hat rolls and crushing the mix with a limiter; 80s mixes keep more dynamics.

@@ era.2.hiphop
**Hip-hop in the 80s.** Drum machines took over: the Roland TR-808 boomed on electro and early rap, and machines like the Oberheim DMX gave hard, punchy kicks and snares. By the late 80s the E-mu SP-1200 and Akai MPC60 let producers sample and chop breaks, setting up the 90s sound.

@@ era.2.trap
**Trap in the 80s.** Trap didn't exist yet, but its key instrument did: the Roland TR-808, heard on 80s electro and early hip-hop. Picking the 80s keeps the modern trap patterns and gives the kit brighter, roomier, machine-like tones, so try a vintage flip: a gated snare and a classic 808 cowbell over modern hat rolls.

@@ era.2.rnb
**R&B in the 80s.** Drum machines moved in: Linn drum machines drove Prince's Minneapolis sound, and Linn and DMX beats with gated snares and claps were all over the decade's R&B. By the late 80s, Teddy Riley's new jack swing mixed swung drum machine beats with hip-hop punch.

@@ era.2.pop
**Pop in the 80s.** Synth pop's big gated snares, drum machines and synth toms defined the decade. The LinnDrum and Oberheim DMX were all over the charts, Phil Collins made gated reverb famous, and even live drummers were compressed, layered and soaked in bright digital reverb.

@@ era.2.rock
**Rock in the 80s.** Big gated snares, huge toms and arena reverb took over, along with the glossy, polished drums of hair metal. Thrash metal went faster and tighter, with double kick drums driving the riffs, and some records layered live drums with samples or Simmons pads.

@@ era.2.country
**Country in the 80s.** Country picked up 80s production, with brighter, compressed drums and some gated reverb, while neotraditional artists like George Strait and Randy Travis kept shuffles and two-step beats alive. Expect polished, punchy, mostly live kits.

@@ era.2.worship
**Worship in the 80s.** Modern worship bands were still to come, but contemporary Christian music picked up the decade's sound, with drum machines, gated snares and synth pads, while gospel choirs kept live drummers and hand claps. Picking the 80s gives the kit brighter, roomier, gated tones for a big, retro praise sound.

@@ era.2.edm
**EDM in the 80s.** Disco lived on in Italo disco and boogie, and in the mid-80s house was born in Chicago and techno in Detroit, built on the Roland TR-808 and TR-909: a four on the floor kick, open hats on the offbeats and claps on 2 and 4. Expect raw, simple machine sounds with little processing.

@@ era.2.lofi
**Lo-fi in the 80s.** Lo-fi hip-hop didn't exist yet, though the samplers behind its dusty sound were just arriving at the end of the decade. Picking the 80s keeps the modern lo-fi patterns but gives the kit brighter, roomier, machine-like tones, so try a vintage flip: a soft, Linn-style kit, gently gated, under warm keys.

@@ era.2.cinematic
**Cinematic drums in the 80s.** Synths and drum machines spread through film scoring, from the synth scores of John Carpenter and Vangelis to electronic action themes like Harold Faltermeyer's "Axel F". Toms got gated and huge. Expect bright, roomy, electronic tones.

@@ era.3
**The 90s sound.** Samplers ruled hip-hop and spread into R&B and pop. Producers chopped drum breaks from old funk and soul records on the E-mu SP-1200 and the Akai MPC60 and MPC3000, often pitched down, gritty and swung. Rock went back to natural, roomy live drums with grunge and alternative, while dance music ran on the TR-909 and on sampled breaks in house, techno, jungle and drum and bass. Mixes got punchier and louder but stayed warm.

### Gear and sounds
- The E-mu SP-1200 (12-bit, gritty, short sample time) and the Akai MPC60 and MPC3000 with their famous swing; the Akai S950 sampler too.
- Two of the most-sampled breaks: James Brown's "Funky Drummer" and the "Amen" break from The Winstons' "Amen, Brother".
- TR-808 and TR-909 sounds in house, techno, Miami bass and Southern rap.
- Big natural rooms and heavy snares in grunge and alt rock.

### Get it in your DAW
- Add 16th-note swing around 54 to 62% and let it do the bounce (new jack swing goes further, close to a full triplet).
- Lower the bit depth (12-bit is a good start) and pitch drums down a few semitones for weight and grit.
- Layer a punchy kick and snare over a break, and low-pass the break so it sits underneath.
- Compress the drum bus hard, but keep reverb short and low.
- Avoid rolling trap hats, long gliding 808s and an over-bright top end.

@@ era.3.hiphop
**Hip-hop in the 90s.** The golden era of boom bap: sampled breaks chopped on the SP-1200 and MPC, swung 16ths, dusty kicks and hard snares. On the West Coast, Dr. Dre's G-funk used cleaner, rolling, laid-back grooves under funk synths, and in Memphis and the South the TR-808 kept booming.

@@ era.3.trap
**Trap in the 90s.** Trap wasn't a named style yet, but its roots were here: Memphis rap and Southern hip-hop leaned on booming TR-808 kicks, rattling hats and dark, sparse beats. Picking the 90s keeps the modern trap patterns but gives the kit punchy, warm, sampler-style tones, so try a vintage flip with gritty 12-bit drums over a modern 808.

@@ era.3.rnb
**R&B in the 90s.** New jack swing ruled the early 90s, then gave way to hip-hop soul, with swung, sampled drums under smooth vocals, and Timbaland's stuttering, syncopated beats for Aaliyah pushed the rhythm forward. From the mid-90s, the neo soul of D'Angelo and Erykah Badu brought back loose, behind-the-beat drums.

@@ era.3.pop
**Pop in the 90s.** Pop drums leaned on R&B and hip-hop, with swung, programmed beats and sampled loops, while teen pop from producers like Max Martin had bright, punchy, tightly programmed kits. Grunge and alt rock also put live, roomy drums back on the radio.

@@ era.3.rock
**Rock in the 90s.** Grunge and alternative brought back big, natural, roomy drums played hard, with Dave Grohl's drumming on Nirvana's records as the classic example. Pop punk sped things up, and by the end of the decade nu metal mixed heavy riffs with hip-hop-style grooves.

@@ era.3.country
**Country in the 90s.** Arena-sized country from Garth Brooks and polished pop-country from Shania Twain gave the drums a big, punchy, radio-ready sound. Line-dance hits kept a steady beat, and honky tonk shuffles and two-steps were still everywhere.

@@ era.3.worship
**Worship in the 90s.** Modern worship began to take shape, with church worship teams like Hillsong's leading songs on a full rock kit, and Kirk Franklin brought hip-hop beats into gospel. Drums were punchy and warm: live kits, tambourine and big builds into the chorus.

@@ era.3.edm
**EDM in the 90s.** Dance music took off: house and techno ran on the TR-909, jungle and drum and bass chopped up the "Amen" break at high tempos, and big beat acts like The Chemical Brothers and Fatboy Slim mixed breaks with rock energy. Trance and UK garage arrived too.

@@ era.3.lofi
**Lo-fi in the 90s.** Lo-fi hip-hop wasn't a genre yet, but its sound comes from here: dusty SP-1200 and MPC boom bap, and jazzy hip-hop from groups like A Tribe Called Quest. Picking the 90s gives the kit punchy, warm, sampler-style tones that fit the modern lo-fi patterns well.

@@ era.3.cinematic
**Cinematic drums in the 90s.** Hans Zimmer and others blended orchestras with synths and big sampled drums, and action scores leaned on driving percussion loops. Picking the 90s gives punchy, warm tones, a good fit for hybrid scores that sound slightly retro.

@@ era.4
**The 2000s sound.** Recording moved into the computer, so drums were edited to the grid, sample-replaced and mixed louder than ever. Hip-hop and R&B went cleaner and harder: Timbaland and The Neptunes made sparse, quirky beats, while crunk and early trap brought back booming 808s. Rock and pop punk drums were tight, bright and often triggered, and masters hit their loudest, with whole mixes squashed by heavy limiting.

### Gear and sounds
- The Akai MPC2000XL and other MPCs, alongside software samplers and drum machines inside the computer.
- Crisp claps, finger snaps and drum machine snares in hip-hop, R&B and pop.
- Sample replacement and triggered kicks in rock and metal for a clicky, even attack.
- Multi-sampled drum software that made realistic live kits possible at home.

### Get it in your DAW
- Quantize tightly and keep swing light, except for neo soul and soulful hip-hop, which still swing more (about 58 to 62%).
- Layer claps with snares, and give kicks a clear click and punch.
- Use 808s with a short to medium decay and few glides.
- Compress and limit firmly for that dense 2000s loudness, but stop before the drums lose their punch.
- Avoid heavy lo-fi grit, big roomy reverb and wall-to-wall hi-hat rolls.

@@ era.4.hiphop
**Hip-hop in the 2000s.** Drums got cleaner and harder: The Neptunes and Timbaland built sparse, quirky beats, Kanye West sped up soul samples over crisp drums, and crunk brought booming 808s and loud claps from the South. J Dilla's loose, unquantized drums shaped soulful hip-hop and, later, lo-fi.

@@ era.4.trap
**Trap in the 2000s.** Trap took shape in Atlanta, with producers like Shawty Redd, DJ Toomp and Zaytoven building it from 808 kicks, snare rolls and rattling hats. The sound was rawer and less polished than today, with shorter 808s and fewer glides.

@@ era.4.rnb
**R&B in the 2000s.** Neo soul leaned back on loose, swung, J Dilla-style drums, while mainstream R&B went crisp and sparse with Timbaland and The Neptunes, using claps, snaps and syncopated kicks. Drums were tight, clean and loud.

@@ era.4.pop
**Pop in the 2000s.** Dance-pop's four on the floor kicks and claps sat alongside R&B-style beats from Timbaland and The Neptunes. Drums were tight, quantized and very loud, with heavy limiting on the whole mix.

@@ era.4.rock
**Rock in the 2000s.** Pop punk, emo and nu metal had tight, bright, often sample-replaced drums edited to the grid, while garage rock revival bands like The White Stripes and The Strokes kept it raw, dry and simple. Loud masters flattened the dynamics of many rock records.

@@ era.4.country
**Country in the 2000s.** Country got louder and more rock-influenced, with tight, bright, punchy live kits and big choruses. Shuffles and train beats still showed up, but straight rock backbeats ruled country radio.

@@ era.4.worship
**Worship in the 2000s.** Band-led modern worship took over many churches, with Chris Tomlin, Hillsong and the Passion movement leading songs on full rock kits. Driving 8th-note kicks and floor toms, big crash builds and delay-heavy guitars defined the sound.

@@ era.4.edm
**EDM in the 2000s.** Electro house, French house and trance filled the clubs, and side-chain pumping (the bass and synths ducking every time the kick hits) became a signature. In London, dubstep was born, with half-time drums around 140 BPM.

@@ era.4.lofi
**Lo-fi in the 2000s.** Lo-fi hip-hop wasn't named yet, but its heroes were at work: J Dilla's loose drums and Nujabes' jazzy beats are its main touchstones. Picking the 2000s gives cleaner, tighter tones, so add your own dust (vinyl noise, a low-pass filter) if you want the classic lo-fi haze.

@@ era.4.cinematic
**Cinematic drums in the 2000s.** Trailer music became its own industry, with huge layered drum hits, taiko-style ensembles and booming impacts, and hybrid scores mixed orchestras with electronic percussion. Picking the 2000s gives tight, loud, clean tones.

@@ era.5
**The 2010s sound.** Laptops and sample packs were everywhere, and trap spread from Atlanta into pop, R&B and even country. Long 808s that glide between notes, fast hi-hat rolls and half-time snares (a snare on beat 3, step 9, so the groove feels half as fast) became the decade's signature. EDM had its festival boom, with huge build-ups, snare rolls and drops, and pop drums turned sparse and punchy, built from claps and snaps. Mixes were crisp, clean and wide.

### Gear and sounds
- Sample packs, and 808 kick-bass samples tuned to the song's key.
- Hi-hat rolls in 16ths, 32nds and triplets.
- Claps and snaps in place of snares in pop and R&B.
- Festival EDM kicks, white-noise risers and snare build-ups, and half-time dubstep beats.

### Get it in your DAW
- Tune the 808 to the key, and glide on some note changes so it carries the bassline.
- Vary hat roll speeds and velocities so they don't sound stiff.
- Keep sounds clean and dry, with a short reverb on claps and snares.
- In dance music, side-chain the bass and pads to the kick so the mix pumps.
- Avoid tape hiss, dusty grit and heavy 90s swing unless you want a throwback.

@@ era.5.hiphop
**Hip-hop in the 2010s.** Trap took over mainstream hip-hop, but boom bap lived on in the underground, and artists like Kendrick Lamar mixed jazz, funk and live drums with modern beats. Drums were crisp and clean, with 808s under almost everything.

@@ era.5.trap
**Trap in the 2010s.** Trap's big decade: Metro Boomin and many others made long, gliding 808s, fast hat rolls and half-time snares the sound of mainstream rap. Along the way, drill and plugg branched off into styles of their own.

@@ era.5.rnb
**R&B in the 2010s.** Alt R&B from The Weeknd, Frank Ocean and others brought dark, sparse, reverb-soaked beats, and trap hats and 808s soon moved in. Trap soul followed, and the backbeat was often a soft snap or rimshot instead of a big snare.

@@ era.5.pop
**Pop in the 2010s.** Dance pop's four on the floor kicks and EDM drops ruled the early 2010s, then trap 808s moved in and drums turned sparse and punchy, built from claps, snaps and tight kicks. Late in the decade, quiet, close-sounding pop like Billie Eilish's used minimal, bass-heavy beats.

@@ era.5.rock
**Rock in the 2010s.** Rock radio got polished and arena-ready, with big stomps and claps from bands like Imagine Dragons, and indie bands mixed live drums with programmed loops. Metal drums were triggered, edited and machine-tight.

@@ era.5.country
**Country in the 2010s.** Bro-country acts like Florida Georgia Line brought programmed loops, snaps and claps in alongside live drums, and Lil Nas X's "Old Town Road", the huge country-rap hit of 2019, put trap drums under a country tune. Drums were bright, clean and compressed.

@@ era.5.worship
**Worship in the 2010s.** Hillsong United, Bethel Music and Elevation Worship led a polished, big-room sound with click tracks, electronic loops under the band, floor tom builds and four on the floor choruses. Fast gospel "chops" fills also spread widely through drummers' online videos.

@@ era.5.edm
**EDM in the 2010s.** The festival boom: big room house, the heavy half-time drops of dubstep from artists like Skrillex, future bass and tropical house. Builds used snare rolls that speed up into the drop, and kicks were huge, clean and loud.

@@ era.5.lofi
**Lo-fi in the 2010s.** Lo-fi hip-hop became a genre of its own, spreading through YouTube streams and playlists made for studying and relaxing. Drums were soft, swung and dusty, with vinyl crackle and low-passed hats. Picking the 2010s gives crisper tones, so add grit to taste.

@@ era.5.cinematic
**Cinematic drums in the 2010s.** The "braam" (a huge brass-like hit made famous by "Inception") and hybrid trailer drums took over, along with ticking pulses, risers and slow, dark covers of pop songs. Percussion was layered, wide and clean.

@@ era.6
**Today's sound.** Every era is on the table at once: producers put dusty breaks, hard 808s and live drums in the same beat, and many records start at home. Drums are polished, loud and hard, with a tight low end and sharp transients, and genres borrow from each other constantly: drill hats in pop, trap 808s in country, Afrobeats and Jersey club rhythms in R&B and pop, and 70s and 80s revival grooves everywhere.

### Gear and sounds
- Distorted, saturated 808s and kicks, with clipping on the drum bus for loudness.
- Sliding 808s and triplet-heavy hats from drill, and distorted rage 808s.
- Phonk cowbells, Jersey club kick runs and Afrobeats percussion.
- Live drums in rock, country and worship, edited tight and layered with samples.

### Get it in your DAW
- Saturate or clip the drum bus for loudness instead of leaning on the limiter alone.
- Keep the low end mono and tight, and tune the kick to the 808 or side-chain them so they don't clash.
- Mix eras on purpose: a dusty break under a clean modern kick sounds current.
- Leave room for the vocal: a few hard hits beat a busy pattern.
- Don't push it so loud that the transients flatten; punch is what sounds modern.

@@ era.6.hiphop
**Hip-hop today.** Everything lives side by side: trap and drill lead the charts, boom bap and jazzy beats have a strong revival, and producers mix sampled breaks with hard 808s. Drums are loud and polished, often clipped on the drum bus for punch.

@@ era.6.trap
**Trap today.** Trap has split into many lanes: hard Atlanta trap, the sliding 808s and syncopated snares of UK and NY drill, the distorted 808s of rage and the soft, melodic bounce of plugg. Drums are crisp, clipped and loud, with 808s tuned tight to the key.

@@ era.6.rnb
**R&B today.** Modern R&B mixes trap-soul 808s and snaps with Afrobeats rhythms and 90s-style throwback grooves, while retro acts like Silk Sonic revived live 70s soul drums. Production is soft, warm and polished, with plenty of space for the voice.

@@ era.6.pop
**Pop today.** Pop borrows from everywhere: soft, close bedroom pop made at home, 80s revival drums like The Weeknd's "Blinding Lights", disco revival, Afrobeats and Jersey club rhythms, and trap 808s. Drums are tight, punchy and very polished, often minimal so the vocal leads.

@@ era.6.rock
**Rock today.** Modern rock mixes are tight, loud and often layered with samples, and some records put live drums over programmed loops and 808s. A pop punk revival brought fast, bright punk beats back to the charts.

@@ era.6.country
**Country today.** Country runs from trap-style drums and 808s under twang to stripped-back, rootsy records from artists like Zach Bryan and Chris Stapleton with warm, live drums. Mainstream country drums are big, clean and compressed, often blending a live kit with loops.

@@ era.6.worship
**Worship today.** Maverick City Music and others brought gospel-influenced live grooves and long, spontaneous sections, while many churches run electronic loops, synth bass and four on the floor builds under a live kit. Trap gospel and electronic worship sit alongside both.

@@ era.6.edm
**EDM today.** Techno leads, from melodic techno to harder, faster styles, with tech house close behind, and drum and bass and UK garage are back in a big way. Drums are tight, punchy and loud, and kicks are sub-heavy, with a rumbling low end in much of techno.

@@ era.6.lofi
**Lo-fi today.** Lo-fi is a huge streaming genre with many lanes, from jazzy chillhop to sad lo-fi trap with soft 808s. Production is cleaner than the dusty originals, with vinyl texture added on purpose. Picking today gives polished tones, so add tape and vinyl character yourself.

@@ era.6.cinematic
**Cinematic drums today.** Hybrid scoring rules: orchestral drums layered with sound-designed hits, sub drops, synth pulses and processed percussion. Trailers lean on huge impacts, a moment of silence before the drop and rhythmic risers. Tones are clean, wide and hard-hitting.
